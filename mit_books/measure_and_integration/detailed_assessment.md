# Detailed Assessment of Statements from Measure and Integration

## Statement 1: Proposition 0.1 (Lecture 1)
Let X, Y, Z be topological spaces such that X -> f -> Y -> g -> Z.
(1) If f and g are continuous, then g o f is continuous.
(2) If f is measurable and g is continuous, then g o f is measurable.

Assessment: included
Part (1) is a basic topology fact present throughout mathlib. Part (2) is captured by `Measurable.comp` and related lemmas in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Defs.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Embedding.lean`. The composition of a measurable function with a continuous (and hence Borel measurable) function is measurable, which is established via `Continuous.measurable` and composition lemmas.

## Statement 2: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 1)
Let u: X -> R, v: X -> R, and Phi: R x R -> Y. Set h(x) = Phi(u(x), v(x)): X -> Y. If u and v are measurable and Phi is continuous, then h: X -> Y is measurable.

Assessment: included
This is a consequence of the fact that measurability is preserved under composition with continuous functions, applied to the product. In mathlib, this follows from `Measurable.prod_mk` (measurability of pairing) and `Continuous.measurable` composed together. See `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Constructions.lean` for product measurability and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Defs.lean` for composition results.

## Statement 3: Proposition 0.3 (Lecture 1)
Let F be any collection of subsets of X. Then there exists a smallest sigma-algebra M* such that F is contained in M*. We call M* the sigma-algebra generated by F.

Assessment: included
This is `MeasurableSpace.generateFrom` in mathlib. The construction of the sigma-algebra generated by a collection of sets is foundational and appears in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Defs.lean`. The function `generateFrom` takes a set of sets and produces the smallest measurable space containing them.

## Statement 4: Proposition 0.1 (Lecture 2)
Let M be a sigma-algebra on X, let Y be a topological space, and let f: X -> Y.
(a) Let Omega be a collection of sets E subset Y such that f^{-1}(E) in M. Then Omega is a sigma-algebra on Y.
(b) If f is measurable and E subset Y is Borel, then f^{-1}(E) in M.
(c) If Y = [-inf, inf] and f^{-1}((alpha, inf]) in M for all alpha, then f is measurable.

Assessment: included
Part (a) follows from general properties of sigma-algebras under preimages. Part (b) is the fundamental property that measurable functions pull back Borel sets to measurable sets, present in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Defs.lean`. Part (c) is captured by results in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Constructions/BorelSpace/Order.lean` which show measurability can be checked on generating sets like half-open intervals.

## Statement 5: Proposition 0.2 (Lecture 2)
A sequence {a_n} converges if and only if lim inf a_n = lim sup a_n = lim a_n.

Assessment: included
This is a standard fact about liminf and limsup in mathlib, available in `Mathlib/Order/LiminfLimsup.lean` and related topology files. The equivalence between convergence and equality of liminf and limsup is established through `Filter.limsup_eq_liminf_iff_le` and related lemmas.

## Statement 6: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 2)
If, for each i = 1, 2, ..., the function f_i: X -> [-inf, inf] is measurable, then g = sup_{i>=1} f_i and h = limsup_{n->inf} f_n are both measurable.

Assessment: included
The measurability of supremum of measurable functions is in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Basic.lean` as `measurable_iSup`. The measurability of limsup follows from the measurability of infimum and supremum. See also `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Constructions/BorelSpace/Order.lean`.

## Statement 7: Corollary 0.4 (Lecture 2)
(a) Pointwise limits of measurable functions are measurable.
(b) If f and g are measurable, then max{f,g} and min{f,g} are measurable.

Assessment: included
Part (a) follows from the measurability of limsup/liminf (Statement 6). Part (b) is `Measurable.max` and `Measurable.min` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Constructions/BorelSpace/Order.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Basic.lean`.

## Statement 8: Theorem 0.5 (Lecture 2)
Let f: X -> [0, inf] be measurable. Then there exists a sequence 0 <= s_1 <= s_2 <= ... of measurable simple functions such that lim_{n->inf} s_n = f.

Assessment: included
This is the approximation theorem for measurable functions by simple functions. In mathlib, this is captured by `MeasureTheory.SimpleFunc.approxOn` and the associated convergence results `tendsto_approxOn` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/SimpleFuncDense.lean`.

## Statement 9: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 3)
A function f is Riemann integrable if and only if f is discontinuous on a set of measure zero.

Assessment: non-included
This is the Lebesgue criterion for Riemann integrability. While mathlib has extensive material on both Riemann and Lebesgue integration, this specific characterization theorem connecting Riemann integrability to the measure of the set of discontinuities does not appear to be formalized in mathlib v4.27.0. Searched for "Riemann" and "discontinuous" in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/` and `Mathlib/Analysis/` without finding this result.

## Statement 10: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 3)
(1) If E subset R and mu(E) < inf, then mu(empty) = 0.
(2) (Monotonicity) A subset B => mu(A) <= mu(B).
(3) If A_i in M, A_1 subset A_2 subset ..., and A = union A_i, then mu(A_i) -> mu(A).
(4) If A_i in M, A_1 supset A_2 supset ..., mu(A_1) < inf, and A = intersection A_i, then mu(A_i) -> mu(A).

Assessment: included
These are fundamental properties of measures, all present in mathlib:
(1) `measure_empty` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean`.
(2) `measure_mono` in the same file.
(3) `tendsto_measure_iUnion` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean`.
(4) `tendsto_measure_iInter` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean`.

## Statement 11: Proposition 0.1 (Lecture 4)
Let s and t be non-negative measurable simple functions. Then integral_X (s+t) dmu = integral_X s dmu + integral_X t dmu.

Assessment: included
The additivity of the integral for simple functions is part of the Lebesgue integral construction in mathlib. See `MeasureTheory.SimpleFunc.lintegral_add` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/Add.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/SimpleFunc.lean`.

## Statement 12: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 4) - Monotone Convergence Theorem
Let f_n: X -> [0, inf] be a sequence of measurable functions such that 0 <= f_1 <= f_2 <= ... and f_n(x) -> f(x) for all x. Then integral_X f_n dmu -> integral_X f dmu.

Assessment: included
This is a cornerstone result in mathlib. It appears as `lintegral_iSup` and related lemmas (`lintegral_tendsto_of_tendsto_of_monotone`) in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/DominatedConvergence.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/Add.lean`.

## Statement 13: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 4)
Let f_n: X -> [0, inf] be measurable and f(x) = sum_{n=1}^inf f_n(x). Then integral_X f dmu = sum_{n=1}^inf integral_X f_n dmu.

Assessment: included
This is `lintegral_tsum` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/Add.lean`. It follows from the monotone convergence theorem applied to partial sums.

## Statement 14: Corollary 0.4 (Lecture 4)
For non-negative a_{ij} with counting measure, sum_{i} sum_{j} a_{ij} = sum_{j} sum_{i} a_{ij}.

Assessment: included
This is a consequence of the interchange of summation and integration (Tonelli/Fubini for sums). In mathlib, this is captured by `tsum_comm` and related results in `Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/InfiniteSum/Basic.lean`, as well as through the counting measure specialization of Fubini's theorem.

## Statement 15: Lemma 0.5 (Lecture 4) - Fatou's Lemma
Let f_n: X -> [0, inf] be measurable. Then integral_X (liminf f_n) dmu <= liminf integral_X f_n dmu.

Assessment: included
This is `lintegral_liminf_le` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/DominatedConvergence.lean`. The Bochner integral version appears as `MeasureTheory.Measure.lintegral_liminf_le` and related results.

## Statement 16: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 5) - Linearity of the Integral
Let f, g in L^1(mu). If alpha, beta in C, then alpha f + beta g in L^1(mu) and integral_X (alpha f + beta g) dmu = alpha integral_X f dmu + beta integral_X g dmu.

Assessment: included
The linearity of the Bochner integral is established in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean` through `integral_add`, `integral_smul`, and related lemmas. The fact that L^1 is closed under linear combinations is part of `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable.lean`.

## Statement 17: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 5)
If f in L^1(mu), then |integral_X f dmu| <= integral_X |f| dmu.

Assessment: included
This is `norm_integral_le_integral_norm` (or `norm_integral_le_lintegral_norm`) in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean`. It is a fundamental inequality for the Bochner integral.

## Statement 18: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 5) - Dominated Convergence Theorem
Let f_n -> f pointwise with |f_n| <= g in L^1. Then f in L^1 and integral f_n -> integral f.

Assessment: included
This is `tendsto_integral_of_dominated_convergence` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean` and also in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/DominatedConvergence.lean`. The Lebesgue integral version exists as well in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/DominatedConvergence.lean`.

## Statement 19: Proposition 0.4 (Lecture 5)
If f ~ g (f = g a.e.), then integral_E f dmu = integral_E g dmu for all E in M.

Assessment: included
This follows from `integral_congr_ae` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean`, which states that if two functions agree almost everywhere, their integrals are equal.

## Statement 20: Theorem 0.5 (Lecture 5) - Completion of a sigma-algebra
The completion construction for a measure space.

Assessment: included
The completion of a measure space is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/NullMeasurable.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean`. The concept of `NullMeasurableSet` and the completion of a measure are well-developed in mathlib.

## Statement 21: Theorem 0.6 (Lecture 5)
(a) If f >= 0 measurable, E in M, integral_E f = 0, then f = 0 a.e. on E.
(b) If f in L^1 and integral_E f = 0 for every E in M, then f = 0 a.e.

Assessment: included
Part (a) is `lintegral_eq_zero_iff` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Lebesgue/Basic.lean`. Part (b) is captured by `ae_eq_zero_of_forall_setIntegral_eq_zero` and related results in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/AEEqOfIntegral.lean`.

## Statement 22: Theorem 0.7 (Lecture 5)
Let E_k in M such that sum mu(E_k) < inf. Then almost every x lies in at most finitely many E_k (Borel-Cantelli lemma).

Assessment: included
This is the first Borel-Cantelli lemma. In mathlib, it appears as `MeasureTheory.measure_limsup_eq_zero` or related results. The condition that sum mu(E_k) < inf implies mu(limsup E_k) = 0 is in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean` and related files.

## Statement 23: Lemma 0.1 (Lecture 7)
Let A, B in L_0. If A and B are disjoint, then A union B in L_0 and lambda(A union B) = lambda(A) + lambda(B).

Assessment: included
This is a basic property of Lebesgue measure (finite additivity for measurable sets). In mathlib, the additivity of measures on disjoint sets is `measure_union` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean`.

## Statement 24: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 7) - Main Approximation Theorem
A in L_0 iff for all epsilon > 0 there exist K compact and G open with K subset A subset G and lambda(G \ K) < epsilon.

Assessment: included
This inner/outer regularity characterization of Lebesgue measurable sets is part of the regularity theory in mathlib. The relevant files are `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Regular.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/Basic.lean`, where Lebesgue measure is shown to be regular.

## Statement 25: Corollary 0.3 (Lecture 7)
If A, B in L_0, then A union B, A intersection B, and A \ B are all in L_0.

Assessment: included
These are basic closure properties of sigma-algebras (and measurable sets with finite outer measure). They follow from the sigma-algebra axioms formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Defs.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean`.

## Statement 26: Corollary 0.5 (Lecture 7)
L (Lebesgue measurable sets) is a sigma-algebra.

Assessment: included
This is fundamental to the construction of Lebesgue measure in mathlib. The Lebesgue measurable sets form a sigma-algebra, which is part of the `MeasurableSpace` instance on R^n. See `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/Basic.lean`.

## Statement 27: Corollary 0.6 (Lecture 7)
lambda is a positive measure on L, and (R^n, L, lambda) is a measure space.

Assessment: included
The Lebesgue measure is constructed as a measure in mathlib in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/Basic.lean`. The measure space instance `MeasureTheory.MeasureSpace` on R^n with Lebesgue measure is well-established.

## Statement 28: Proposition 0.4 (Lecture 7)
The extended Lebesgue measure is consistent with the restricted one.

Assessment: included
The consistency of the Lebesgue measure construction (extension from sets of finite measure to all measurable sets) is part of the measure construction in mathlib, handled through `OuterMeasure` and `Measure.ofOuterMeasure` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpace.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/OuterMeasure/OfAddContent.lean`.

## Statement 29: Corollary 0.1 (Lecture 9)
If A subset R^n is measurable with positive measure, then there exists B subset A that is not measurable.

Assessment: non-included
This statement about the existence of non-measurable subsets of any set with positive measure is a consequence of the Vitali construction. While mathlib has basic measure theory infrastructure, this specific result about non-measurable subsets does not appear to be formalized. Searched for "NonMeasurable", "non_measurable", and "Vitali" set constructions in the measure theory directory without finding this exact statement.

## Statement 30: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 9) - Invariance under linear transformations
Let T: R^n -> R^n be a linear map. Then lambda*(TA) = |det T| lambda*(A), and if A is measurable, lambda(TA) = |det T| lambda(A).

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/EqHaar.lean` as `addHaar_image_linearMap` and related results. The fact that Lebesgue measure transforms by |det T| under linear maps is a key result in mathlib's measure theory.

## Statement 31: Lemma 0.3 (Lecture 9)
Let T be invertible and J = [0,1)^n. The measure lambda(TJ) = rho * lambda(J) determines the transformation factor for all sets.

Assessment: included
This is an intermediate step in the proof of Statement 30 and is implicit in the construction in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/EqHaar.lean`. The key idea that the transformation factor is determined by the image of a single cube is part of the proof strategy used in mathlib.

## Statement 32: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 10) - Riesz Representation Theorem
Let X be a locally compact Hausdorff space. Let Lambda: C_c(X) -> C be a positive linear functional. Then there exists a unique regular Borel measure mu such that Lambda f = integral_X f dmu for all f in C_c(X).

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/RieszMarkovKakutani/Basic.lean`, `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/RieszMarkovKakutani/Real.lean`, and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/RieszMarkovKakutani/NNReal.lean`. The Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem is fully formalized in mathlib.

## Statement 33: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 11) - Lusin's Theorem
Let f be measurable on a locally compact Hausdorff space X with mu(A) < inf. Given epsilon > 0, there exists g in C_c(X) such that mu({x | f(x) != g(x)}) < epsilon.

Assessment: included
Lusin's theorem is formalized in mathlib. The relevant file is `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Constructions/Polish/Basic.lean` which contains results about measurable functions being nearly continuous. See also the Lusin-related results accessible through the measure theory library.

## Statement 34: Corollary 0.2 (Lecture 11)
Under the hypotheses of Lusin's theorem with f bounded, there exists a sequence g_n in C_c(X) with |g_n| < M and f = lim g_n a.e.

Assessment: included
This follows from Lusin's theorem and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, both of which are in mathlib. The approximation of bounded measurable functions by continuous functions with compact support is implicit in the density results in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ContinuousMapDense.lean`.

## Statement 35: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 11) - Vitali-Caratheodory Theorem
Let f in L^1(mu). Given epsilon > 0, there exist u (upper semicontinuous) and v (lower semicontinuous) with u <= f <= v and integral (v - u) < epsilon.

Assessment: included
The Vitali-Caratheodory theorem is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/VitaliCaratheodory.lean`. This file contains the approximation of integrable functions by semicontinuous functions.

## Statement 36: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 12) - LDCT for a.e. convergence
The dominated convergence theorem holds when convergence and domination are only assumed almost everywhere.

Assessment: included
The a.e. version of dominated convergence is the standard version in mathlib. See `tendsto_integral_of_dominated_convergence` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/DominatedConvergence.lean`, which state the theorem with a.e. hypotheses.

## Statement 37: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 12)
If sum integral |f_k| < inf, then sum f_k converges a.e. and the integral of the sum equals the sum of the integrals.

Assessment: included
This follows from the dominated convergence theorem and monotone convergence in mathlib. The relevant results include `hasSum_integral_of_summable` and related lemmas. The absolute convergence of the series implies applicability of dominated convergence, which is standard in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean`.

## Statement 38: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 13) - Egoroff's Theorem
If mu(X) < inf and f_n -> f a.e., then for all epsilon > 0, there exists E with mu(X \ E) < epsilon and f_n -> f uniformly on E.

Assessment: included
Egoroff's theorem is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/Egorov.lean` (note the different spelling). The theorem `MeasureTheory.Egorov` or `tendstoUniformlyOn_of_ae_tendsto` provides this result.

## Statement 39: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 13)
If f_n -> f a.e. and mu(X) < inf, then f_n -> f in measure.

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ConvergenceInMeasure.lean`. The result that a.e. convergence implies convergence in measure (for finite measure spaces) is `TendstoInMeasure.of_tendsto_ae` or a similar name.

## Statement 40: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 13)
If f_n -> f in measure, then there is a subsequence f_{n_k} -> f a.e.

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ConvergenceInMeasure.lean` as a result about extracting a.e. convergent subsequences from sequences converging in measure.

## Statement 41: Theorem 0.4 (Lecture 13) - DCT for convergence in measure
Dominated convergence holds with convergence in measure replacing pointwise convergence.

Assessment: included
This extension of DCT to convergence in measure is available in mathlib through the combination of the subsequence extraction result (Statement 40) and the standard DCT. See `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ConvergenceInMeasure.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/DominatedConvergence.lean`.

## Statement 42: Lemma 0.5 (Lecture 13)
If every subsequence of a_n has a subsequence converging to alpha, then a_n -> alpha.

Assessment: included
This is a general topological/metric space fact present in mathlib as `tendsto_of_subseq_tendsto` or similar results in `Mathlib/Topology/Sequences.lean` and related files.

## Statement 43: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 14) - Continuity of convex functions
If phi is convex on (a,b), then phi is continuous on (a,b).

Assessment: included
This is formalized in mathlib in the convexity library. The continuity of convex functions on open intervals is available in `Mathlib/Analysis/Convex/Function.lean` or `Mathlib/Analysis/Convex/Topology.lean` as `ConvexOn.continuousOn` or related results.

## Statement 44: Jensen's Inequality (Lecture 14)
Let mu be a probability measure. If phi is convex and f in L^1, then phi(integral f dmu) <= integral (phi o f) dmu.

Assessment: included
Jensen's inequality for integrals is formalized in mathlib. It appears in the context of conditional expectations in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ConditionalExpectation/Real.lean` and also through more general convexity results. The integral version of Jensen's inequality is available via `ConvexOn.integral_comp_le` or similar.

## Statement 45: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 14) - Holder's and Minkowski's Inequalities
Holder's inequality: integral fg <= (integral f^p)^{1/p} (integral g^q)^{1/q}.
Minkowski's inequality: (integral (f+g)^p)^{1/p} <= (integral f^p)^{1/p} + (integral g^p)^{1/p}.

Assessment: included
Both inequalities are formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/MeanInequalities.lean`. Holder's inequality appears as `NNReal.inner_le_iff` and related Lp versions. Minkowski's inequality is `snorm_add_le` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSeminorm/TriangleInequality.lean`.

## Statement 46: Proposition 0.1 (Lecture 15)
If f in L^inf, then |f(x)| <= ||f||_inf a.e.

Assessment: included
This is a basic property of the essential supremum norm. In mathlib, it follows from the definition of `snormEssSup` and properties of `essSup` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSeminorm/Basic.lean`.

## Statement 47: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 15) - Holder's inequality for L^p
Let 1 <= p <= inf and 1/p + 1/q = 1. If f in L^p and g in L^q, then fg in L^1 and ||fg||_1 <= ||f||_p ||g||_q.

Assessment: included
This general form of Holder's inequality (including the endpoint cases p=1,inf) is in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Integral/MeanInequalities.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSeminorm/Basic.lean`. See `MeasureTheory.snorm_mul_le` and related results.

## Statement 48: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 15) - Minkowski's inequality (triangle inequality)
Let 1 <= p <= inf. If f, g in L^p, then f + g in L^p and ||f + g||_p <= ||f||_p + ||g||_p.

Assessment: included
This is `snorm_add_le` in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSeminorm/TriangleInequality.lean`. It establishes the triangle inequality for the L^p seminorm.

## Statement 49: Lemma 0.4 (Lecture 15)
If {f_n} is Cauchy, then there exists a subsequence f_{n_k} with d(f_{n_{k+1}}, f_{n_k}) <= 2^{-k}.

Assessment: included
This is a standard lemma about Cauchy sequences in metric spaces, present in mathlib's general theory of metric spaces. It is used in the proof of completeness of L^p and is available through `Metric.CauchySeq` related results.

## Statement 50: Theorem 0.5 (Lecture 15) - Riesz-Fischer Theorem
For 1 <= p <= inf, L^p(mu) is a Banach space (complete normed space).

Assessment: included
The completeness of L^p spaces is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSpace/Complete.lean`. The `Lp` type in mathlib is shown to be a complete normed space, establishing the Riesz-Fischer theorem.

## Statement 51: Theorem 0.6 (Lecture 15)
A Cauchy sequence in L^p converging in norm has a subsequence converging pointwise a.e.

Assessment: included
This follows from the proof of completeness of L^p. In mathlib, the extraction of a.e. convergent subsequences from L^p convergent sequences is part of the theory in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSpace/Complete.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ConvergenceInMeasure.lean`.

## Statement 52: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 16) - Simple functions dense in L^p
The set of simple measurable functions with finite support measure is dense in L^p for 1 <= p < inf.

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/SimpleFuncDenseLp.lean`. The density of simple functions in L^p spaces is a key structural result used in the construction of the Bochner integral.

## Statement 53: Corollary 0.2 (Lecture 16) - C_c dense in L^p
If X is a locally compact Hausdorff space, then C_c(X) is dense in L^p for 1 <= p < inf.

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/ContinuousMapDense.lean`. The density of continuous functions with compact support in L^p spaces is established for locally compact spaces with regular measures.

## Statement 54: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 16) - Completion of C_c under sup norm is C_0
The completion of C_c(X) under the sup norm is C_0(X).

Assessment: included
This is a standard result in functional analysis. In mathlib, the relationship between C_c and C_0 is developed in `Mathlib/Topology/ContinuousMap/ZeroAtInfty.lean` and related files, where C_0 is shown to be the closure/completion of C_c under the uniform norm.

## Statement 55: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 17) - Limit of L^p norms
If f in L^r for some r < inf, then lim_{p->inf} ||f||_p = ||f||_inf.

Assessment: included
This is formalized in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSeminorm/CompareExp.lean`. The convergence of L^p norms to the L^inf norm as p -> inf is established through `snorm_exponent_top` and related results.

## Statement 56: Theorem 0.2 (Lecture 17) - L^q subset L^p for finite measure spaces
If mu(X) < inf and 1 <= p < q < inf, then L^q subset L^p.

Assessment: included
This inclusion for finite measure spaces is in `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/LpSeminorm/CompareExp.lean` and `Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable.lean`. The result `Memℒp.memℒp_of_exponent_le` or similar captures this inclusion when the measure is finite.

## Statement 57: Theorem 0.3 (Lecture 17) - l^p inclusions
For counting measure: if 1 <= p < q <= inf, then l^p subset l^q and ||f||_inf <= ||f||_q <= ||f||_p <= ||f||_1.

Assessment: included
The inclusion relations for l^p spaces (based on counting measure) are available in mathlib through the general L^p theory specialized to counting measure, as well as through `Mathlib/Analysis/NormedSpace/lp.lean` and related files.

## Statement 58: Theorem 0.4 (Lecture 17) - Interpolation inequality for L^p norms

## Statement 59: Theorem 0.1 (Lecture 18) - Fubini I (non-negative functions / Tonelli)
