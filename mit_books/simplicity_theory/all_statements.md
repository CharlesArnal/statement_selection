# All Mathematical Statements in Simplicity Theory (OCR'd Lecture Notes)

## Chapter 1: Basic Definitions and Framework

**Fact 1 (Universal Domain Fact).**
Assume U is a universal domain. If a is in U and phi(x,y) is a formula such that U satisfies phi(a,y), then there is a formula psi(x) in Delta such that U satisfies psi(a) and U satisfies forall x (psi(x) -> not exists y phi(x,y)).

**Remark 1 (Existential Closure of Universal Domain).**
If U is a universal domain with respect to Delta, and Delta' = exists-Delta, then U is a universal domain with respect to Delta'.

**Fact 2 (S_n(T) as Maximal Consistent Types).**
S_n(T) = {tp(a) : a in U^n} equals the set of maximal consistent sets of formulas in n variables consistent with T. The logic topology on S_n(T) (generated by sets [phi] = {p : phi in p}) is a compact topology.

**Lemma 1 (Extraction of Indiscernible Sequences via Erdos-Rado).**
Let A be a subset of U with |S_k(A)| <= lambda. Set mu = beth_{lambda^+}. For each sequence (a_i : i < mu) of k-tuples in U, there exists (b_i : i < omega) in U^k such that (b_i : i < omega) is A-indiscernible and for all n < omega there exist i_0 < ... < i_{n-1} < mu such that tp(a_{i_0},...,a_{i_{n-1}}/A) = tp(b_0,...,b_{n-1}/A).

**Easy Fact (Extension of Indiscernible Sequences).**
Assume (a_i : i < omega) is A-indiscernible. Then for all lambda >= omega there is an A-indiscernible sequence (b_i : i < lambda) similar to (a_i); moreover, tp(b_i : i < lambda / A) is determined by tp((a_i) / A).

**Corollary 1 (Extension/Extraction for Indiscernible Sequences).**
Let A be a subset of B. Let (a_i : i < omega) be an A-indiscernible sequence. Then there exists a B-indiscernible sequence (b_i : i < omega) which is similar to (a_i) over A.

## Chapter 2: Dividing and Independence

**Lemma 2 (Dividing Reduces to a Single Formula).**
A partial type p(x,b) divides over c iff there is a formula phi(x,b) in p(x,b) which divides over c.

**Corollary 2 (Finite Character of Independence).**
a is independent from b over c (where a, b, c are possibly infinite tuples) if and only if for all finite subtuples a' of a and b' of b, a' is independent from b' over c.

**Corollary 3 (Downward Right-Hand Transitivity).**
For all a, b, c, d: a is independent from b,d over c implies a is independent from b over c and a is independent from d over c.

**Corollary 4 (Upward Left-Hand Transitivity).**
a is independent from b over c and d is independent from b over c implies ad is independent from b over c.

**Proposition 1 (Dividing Characterized by k-Inconsistency Witnesses).**
(1) phi(x,b) divides over c iff (2) divides over c with respect to some k-inconsistency witness psi iff (3) there exists a c-indiscernible sequence (b_i) in tp(b/c) such that psi(b_0,...,b_{k-1}) holds.

## Chapter 3: Simplicity

**Theorem 1 (Characterization of Simplicity: TFAE).**
The following are equivalent:
(1) T is simple (i.e., kappa^0(T) < infinity).
(2) For all (phi, psi) in Xi, there exists l < omega such that there is no sequence (b_i : i < l) where each phi(x,b_i) divides over b_{<i} and the conjunction of phi(x,b_i) is consistent.
(3) For every p, D(p, Xi) is a set (contains no infinite chains of a single pair).
(4) For every p, |D(p, Xi)| <= |T|^+.

**Theorem 2 (D-rank Characterization of Non-Dividing).**
Let p = tp(a/b) and q = tp(a/bc). The following are equivalent:
(1) D(p, Xi) = D(q, Xi).
(2) Every maximal element of D(p, Xi) is also in D(q, Xi).
(3) q does not divide over b.

**Proposition 2 (Universality of Morley Sequences).**
Assume (b_i : i < omega) is a Morley sequence over c and it is also indiscernible over ac. Then a is independent from b_0 over c.

**Lemma 3 (Morley Sequence Independence).**
Assume (a_i : i < omega) is a Morley sequence over c. Then for all n < omega, a_n is independent from a_{<n} over c.

**Lemma 4 (Existence of Morley Sequences).**
For all a, c there exists a Morley sequence (a_i : i < omega) over c which is in tp(a/c).

**Corollary 5 (Characterization of Dividing via Morley Sequences).**
Let p(x,b) be a partial type over b. Then p(x,b) divides over c iff for all Morley sequences (b_i) for b over c, the conjunction of p(x,b_i) is inconsistent, iff for some Morley sequence (b_i) for b over c, the conjunction of p(x,b_i) is inconsistent.

**Theorem 3 (Properties of Independence in Thick Simple Theories).**
Let T be a thick simple theory. Then independence satisfies:
1. Automorphism invariance
2. Finite character
3. Symmetry
4. Transitivity
5. Extension
6. Local character
7. The Independence Theorem.
Moreover, if I is any notion of independence satisfying properties 1-6, then T is simple and I equals non-dividing independence.

**Corollary 6 (Improved Extension).**
Assume a is independent from b over c and d is given. Then there exists a' with a' equivalent to a over c such that a' is independent from bd over c.

**Corollary 7 (Symmetry of Independence).**
a is independent from b over c if and only if b is independent from a over c.

**Corollary 8 (Transitivity of Independence).**
Follows from right downward transitivity, left upward transitivity, and symmetry.

**Lemma 5 (Indiscernible Sequence Implies Independence).**
Assume (a_i : i < omega) is c-indiscernible. Then a_omega is independent from a_{<omega} over c.

**Lemma 6 (Indiscernible Sequence Yields Morley Sequence).**
Let (a_i : i < 2*omega) be a c-indiscernible sequence. Then (a_{omega+i} : i < omega) is a Morley sequence over c union a_{<omega}.

**Corollary 9 (Non-Dividing and Consistent Union).**
Assume p(x,b,c) does not divide over c. Let (b_i) be c-indiscernible in tp(b/c). Then the union of p(x,b_i,c) is consistent and does not divide over c.

**Improved Improved Extension.**
If p(x,bc) is a partial type over bc and does not divide over c, then it can be extended to a complete type over bc that does not divide over c.

## Chapter 4: Lascar Strong Types

**Lemma 7 (Characterization of Lascar Strong Type Equality).**
a =^{Ls}_A b if and only if d_A(a,b) < infinity, where d_A(a,b) is the least n such that there are a = a_0, a_1,..., a_n = b where each (a_i, a_{i+1}) starts an infinite A-indiscernible sequence.

**Lemma 8 (Extension for Strong Types).**
(T simple) Let A be a subset of B and let a be a tuple. Then there exists a tuple a' such that a' =^{Ls}_A a and a' is independent from B over A.

**Lemma 9 (Amalgamation over Lascar Strong Types).**
(T simple) Let p_1(x,a) and p_2(x,b) be partial types over Aa and Ab respectively. Assume b =^{Ls}_A b' and a is independent from bb'. Also assume that p_1(x,a) union p_2(x,b) does not divide over A. Then p_1(x,a) union p_2(x,b') does not divide over A.

**Corollary 10 (The Independence Theorem).**
Let A be a set. Let b_1, b_2 be tuples with b_1 is independent from b_2 over A. Let a_1, a_2 be tuples with a_1 =^{Ls}_A a_2, a_i is independent from b_i (i=1,2). Then there exists a tuple a such that a =^{Ls}_A a_i, a is independent from b_1 b_2 over A, and a is equivalent to a_i over Ab_i (i=1,2).

**Corollary 11 (Strong Type Extension with Type Preservation).**
We could require a to be equivalent to a_i over Ab_i for i=1,2 in the independence theorem.

**Corollary 12 (Morley Sequences from Equal Lascar Strong Types).**
Let A be a set, a_0, a_1 tuples such that a_0 =^{Ls}_A a_1 and a_0 is independent from a_1 over A. Then a_0, a_1 start a Morley sequence over A.

**Corollary 13 (d_A Bound for Equal Lascar Strong Types).**
a =^{Ls}_A b implies d_A(a,b) <= 2.

**Corollary 14 (Type-Definability of Lascar Strong Type Equality).**
Equality of Lascar strong types is type-definable: there exists E(x,y) over A such that E(a,b) iff a =^{Ls}_A b.

**Corollary 15 (D-rank Characterization of Independence).**
a is independent from b over c if and only if D(a/c, Xi) = D(a/bc, Xi).

**Important Corollary (Definable Independence).**
Given any a, c, and Xi, there is a partial type p(y,a,c) such that for b of appropriate length, a is independent from b over c iff b satisfies p(y,a,c).

## Chapter 5: Definable Independence and Products

**Proposition 3 (Product of Types with Definable Independence).**
Assume that p(x) and q(y) have definable independence over c. Then there exists r(x,y) := p(x) tensor_c q(y) such that a,b satisfy r implies a satisfies p, b satisfies q, and a is independent from b over c. Moreover, r has definable independence over c.

**Lemma 10 (Morley Sequence Automorphic Images Remain Morley).**
Assume (a_i) is a Morley sequence over c in p = tp(a_0/c). Let (a_i') be an automorphic image of (a_i), also c-indiscernible in p. Then (a_i') is also a Morley sequence over c.

## Chapter 6: Hyperimaginaries

**Lemma 11 (Equivalence of Hyperimaginary Type Equality: TFAE).**
For fixed a, b:
(i) tp(a_E) = tp(b_E)
(ii) There exists f in Aut(U) such that a_E = f(b)_E
(iii) There exist c equivalent to a under E, d equivalent to b under E, such that c = d.

**Fact 3 (Simplicity Transfers to Hyperimaginaries).**
U is simple if and only if U^{heq} (the expansion with all hyperimaginary sorts) is simple.

**Lemma 12 (Hyperimaginary Codes for Bounded Type-Definable Sets).**
Bounded type-definable sets of hyperimaginaries have hyperimaginary "codes" (canonical parameters). Namely if p(x) is a partial type with parameters a, and B = {b : p(b,a)} is bounded, then there exists c such that an automorphism fixes c iff it fixes B setwise.

**Lemma 13 (Hyperimaginaries Reduce to Small Ones).**
Every hyperimaginary is interdefinable with a tuple of "small" hyperimaginaries, where small means quotient of a type of length at most |T|.

## Chapter 7: Canonical Bases and Amalgamation

**Proposition 4 (Characterization of R-tilde).**
a R-tilde b iff a E b and a is independent from b over a_E. Moreover, D(a/a_E, Xi) = D(tp(a) intersect R(x,b), Xi) = D(a/b, Xi).

**Claim (R* Equals R^2).**
The transitive closure R* of R equals R^2 (the 2-iterate of R).

**Corollary 16 (R* is Type-Definable).**
The transitive closure R* is type-definable.

**Theorem 4 (Properties of Canonical Bases).**
With previous conventions and notations:
(1) a_E (= Cb(p(x,a))) is a canonical parameter for the parallelism class of p(x,a): an automorphism f in Aut(U) fixes a_E iff it fixes P/|| setwise.
(2) p(x,a) does not divide over a_E.
(3) p(x,a)|_{a_E} is an amalgamation base.
(4) If b is in bdd(a) and p(x,a) does not divide over b, then a_E is in bdd(b). If moreover p(x,a)|_b is an amalgamation base then a_E is in dcl(b).

## Chapter 8: Kim-Pillay Theorem

**Theorem 5 (Cameron's Theorem / Kim-Pillay Characterization).**
A complete theory T is simple if and only if it has an independence relation satisfying: invariance, existence, finite character, monotonicity, transitivity, local character, extension, symmetry, and the independence theorem. In this case, the independence relation equals non-dividing.

## Chapter 9: SU-Rank

**Lemma 14 (Characterization of Supersimplicity: TFAE).**
(1) For every type p (in finitely many variables), SU(p) < infinity.
(2) For every type p in a single variable, SU(p) < infinity.
(3) For every singleton a and set A, there exists a finite A_0 subset of A such that a is independent from A over A_0.
(4) For every finite a and set A, there exists a finite A_0 subset of A such that a is independent from A over A_0.

**Lemma 15 (SU-rank and Non-Dividing).**
Assume p is contained in q where p is in S(A), q is in S(B), A subset of B. Then:
(1) SU(q) <= SU(p).
(2) SU(p) >= alpha implies SU(q) >= alpha (if a is independent from B over A).
(3) If SU(q) = SU(p) < infinity then q does not divide over A.

**Lascar Inequality I.**
SU(a/Ab) + SU(b/A) <= SU(ab/A) <= SU(a/bA) oplus SU(b/A), where oplus denotes symmetric (natural/Hessenberg) addition of ordinals.

**Lascar Inequality II.**
If a is independent from b over A, then SU(ab/A) = SU(a/A) oplus SU(b/A).

**Lascar Inequality III (Higher Exponent Symmetry).**
Assume SU(a/A) >= SU(a/Ab) + omega^alpha * n. Then SU(b/A) >= SU(b/Aa) + omega^alpha * n.

## Chapter 10: Stability

**Theorem 6 (Stability Characterization: TFAE).**
(1) T is stable.
(2) Every type is definable.
(3) For all A, |S(A)| <= (|A| + |T|)^{|T|}.
(4) There exists lambda >= |T| such that for all A with |A| <= lambda, |S(A)| <= lambda.

**Theorem 7 (Properties of Definable Types over Saturated Models).**
Let M be |T|^+-saturated, p in S(M) definable. Then:
(i) p has a unique definition (up to equivalence).
(ii) The unique definition is good.
(iii) For all B containing M, p|_B is a nonsplitting extension of p.

**Remark 2 (Good Definitions Imply Non-Dividing).**
Assume p in S(A) has a good definition. Then for all B containing A, p|_B does not divide over A.

**Remark 3 (Nonsplitting Extensions Imply Lascar Strong).**
Assume that p in S(A) has nonsplitting extensions to every set B containing A. Then p is Lascar strong.

**Corollary 17 (Stable Types over Saturated Models are Lascar Strong).**
(T stable) Every type over a |T|^+-saturated model is Lascar strong.

## Chapter 11: Stability Implies Simplicity, Stationarity

**Theorem 8 (Characterization of Stability via Stationarity: TFAE).**
(1) T is stable.
(2) T is simple and Lascar strong types are stationary (have unique non-dividing extension to every set).
(3) T is simple and every type has a bounded multiplicity.

**Proposition 5 (Cofinal Class Criterion for Simplicity).**
Assume that for every finite type a, for every increasing sequence (A_i : i < |T|^+) in a cofinal class A, a is independent from A_j over A_i for some i < j. Then T is simple.

## Chapter 12: Consequences for Saturated Models

**Consequence 1 (Types over Saturated Models are Lascar Strong).**
If M is |T|^+-saturated, then types over M are Lascar strong. In fact: if a is equivalent to b over M then d_M(a,b) <= 2.

**Consequence 2 (Realization of Lascar Strong Types in Saturated Models).**
If A is a subset of M and M is (|A|+|T|)^+-saturated, then all Lascar strong types (of finite tuples) over A are realized in M.

**Consequence 3 (Co-heir Property).**
Assume a is finite, M is |T|^+-saturated, B contains M, T is stable. Then a is independent from B over M iff for all A subset of B with |A| <= |T|, tp(a/A) is realized in M.

## Chapter 13: Canonical Bases and Stationarity in Stable Theories

**Theorem 9 (Canonical Bases in Stable First-Order Theories).**
If T is stable and first order, then for stationary p, the family {d_phi p} (canonical parameter for the phi-definition of p) forms a canonical base for p. An automorphism fixes Cb(p) iff it fixes the definition iff it fixes p/|| setwise.

**Corollary 18 (tp(a/acl^{eq}(A)) is Lascar Strong).**
Let T be stable. Then tp(a/acl^{eq}(A)) is Lascar strong.

## Chapter 14: PAPA and Automorphisms

**Theorem 10 (PAPA for Stable Theories).**
T stable implies T has the PAPA (Propriete d'Amalgamation de Paires d'Automorphismes) over models.

**Theorem 10' (PAPA over Algebraically Closed Sets).**
T stable has the PAPA over algebraically closed sets.

**Theorem 10'' (PAPA for Stable CATs).**
T a stable CAT has PAPA over |T|^+-saturated models.

## Chapter 15: Model Companion of T with an Automorphism

**Theorem 11 (Properties of T_A).**
Let T be a first order stable theory. Let L_sigma = L union {sigma} where sigma is a unary function symbol. Let T_0 = T union "sigma is an automorphism". Assume that T_0 has a model companion T_A. Then:
(1) T_A is simple.
(2) If M satisfies T_A and a, b, c are in M, then a is independent from b over c in the sense of T_A iff acl_sigma(a,c) is independent from acl_sigma(b,c) union acl_sigma(c) in the sense of T.
Moreover, T_A is stable iff for T, algebraically closed sets A, B have algebraically closed join dcl^{eq}(A union B).

## Chapter 16: Random Graph

**Fact 4 (Random Graph Properties).**
(I) T_1 (the theory of the random graph) is complete and has quantifier elimination; it is omega-categorical.
(II) T_1 is the model completion of T_0.
(III) T_1 is simple, and independence is: A is independent from B over C iff A intersect (B union C) = A intersect C (i.e., B intersect A is contained in C).

## Chapter 17: Hilbert Space Example

**Claim (Independence in Hilbert Space).**
In the Hilbert space universal domain, independence A is independent from B over C means P_C(A) = P_{CB}(A) (where P denotes orthogonal projection). This satisfies all the axioms of a simple independence relation.

**Lemma 16 (Unique Orthogonal Extension in Hilbert Space).**
Every tp(A/C) has a unique orthogonal extension to a type over CB (in the Hilbert space setting).

## Chapter 18: Thin Formulas and Trees

**Fact 5 (Thin Formula Characterization of d_A).**
d_A(a,b) <= 1 iff a, b satisfy no thin formula over A. (A formula phi(x,y) is thin if the conjunction of phi(x_i, x_j) for i < j < omega is inconsistent.)

## Chapter 19: The Freeer Theorem (Independence Theorem over Cofinal Classes)

**Theorem 12 (Independence Theorem over Cofinal Classes).**
The Independence Theorem holds even if we only assume it for types over distinguished sets, provided the class of distinguished sets is cofinal.
