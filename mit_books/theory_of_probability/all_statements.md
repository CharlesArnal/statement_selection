# All Mathematical Statements: Theory of Probability

## Statement 1
**Theorem (Measures on R).** For each right continuous, non-decreasing function F, tending to 0 at $-\infty$ and to 1 at $\infty$, there is a unique measure defined on the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}$ with $P((a, b]) = F(b) - F(a)$.
(Line 140)

## Statement 2
**Theorem (pi-lambda theorem).** If $\mathcal{P}$ is a $\pi$-system and $\mathcal{L}$ is a $\lambda$-system that contains $\mathcal{P}$, then $\sigma(\mathcal{P}) \subset \mathcal{L}$, where $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$ denotes smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{A}$.
(Line 168)

## Statement 3
**Theorem (Caratheodory Extension Theorem).** If $\mu$ is a $\sigma$-finite measure on an algebra $\mathcal{A}$ then $\mu$ has a unique extension to the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal{A}$.
(Line 172)

## Statement 4
**Theorem (Measures on R^d).** Given F, there is a unique measure whose values on finite rectangles are determined by $F(x) = \mu(-\infty, x_1] \times (-\infty, x_2] \times \ldots \times (-\infty, x_n]$ (provided that F is non-decreasing, right continuous, and assigns a non-negative value to each rectangle).
(Line 230)

## Statement 5
**Theorem (Measurability via generators).** If $X^{-1}(A) \in \mathcal{F}$ for all $A \in \mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{A}$ generates $\mathcal{S}$, then X is a measurable map from $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ to $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{S})$.
(Line 243)

## Statement 6
**Theorem (Properties of integration).** If $f$ and $g$ are integrable then: (1) If $f \ge 0$ a.s. then $\int f d\mu \ge 0$. (2) For $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$, have $\int (af+bg)d\mu = a\int fd\mu + b\int gd\mu$. (3) If $g \le f$ a.s. then $\int g d\mu \le \int f d\mu$. (4) If $g = f$ a.e. then $\int g d\mu = \int f d\mu$. (5) $|\int f d\mu| \le \int |f| d\mu$.
(Line 317, repeated at 376)

## Statement 7
**Jensen's inequality.** If $\mu$ is probability measure and $\phi : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is convex then $\phi(\int f d\mu) \leq \int \phi(f) d\mu$. If X is random variable then $E\phi(X) \geq \phi(EX)$.
(Line 396)

## Statement 8
**Holder's inequality.** Write $||f||_p = (\int |f|^p d\mu)^{1/p}$ for $1 \le p < \infty$. If $1/p + 1/q = 1$, then $\int |fg| d\mu \le ||f||_p ||g||_q$.
(Line 399)

## Statement 9
**Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.** Special case $p = q = 2$. Gives $\int |fg|d\mu \le ||f||_2 ||g||_2$. Says that dot product of two vectors is at most product of vector lengths.
(Line 401)

## Statement 10
**Bounded convergence theorem.** Consider probability measure $\mu$ and suppose $|f_n| \leq M$ a.s. for all n and some fixed $M > 0$, and that $f_n \to f$ in probability (i.e., $\lim_{n \to \infty} \mu\{x : |f_n(x) - f(x)| > \epsilon\} = 0$ for all $\epsilon > 0$). Then $\int f d\mu = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int f_n d\mu$.
(Line 407)

## Statement 11
**Fatou's lemma.** If $f_n \ge 0$ then $\liminf_{n\to\infty} \int f_n d\mu \geq \int (\liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n) d\mu$.
(Line 419)

## Statement 12
**Monotone convergence theorem.** If $f_n \ge 0$ and $f_n \uparrow f$ then $\int f_n d\mu \uparrow \int f d\mu$.
(Line 431)

## Statement 13
**Dominated convergence theorem.** If $f_n \to f$ a.e. and $|f_n| \le g$ for all n and g is integrable, then $\int f_n d\mu \to \int f d\mu$.
(Line 436)

## Statement 14
**Theorem (Strong law of large numbers).** If $X_1, X_2, ...$ are i.i.d. real-valued random variables with expectation m and $A_n := n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ are the empirical means then $\lim_{n\to\infty} A_n = m$ almost surely.
(Line 482, repeated at 560, 933, 1021)

## Statement 15
**Theorem (Independence and pi-systems).** If $A_1, A_2, ..., A_n$ are independent, and each $A_i$ is a $\pi$-system, then $\sigma(A_1), ..., \sigma(A_n)$ are independent.
(Line 511)

## Statement 16
**Theorem (Standard Borel spaces).** If S is a complete separable metric space M (or a Borel subset of such a space) and S is the set of Borel subsets of S, then (S, S) is nice (i.e., there exists a 1-1 map from S to $\mathbb{R}$ so that $\phi$ and $\phi^{-1}$ are both measurable).
(Line 585)

## Statement 17
**Fubini's theorem.** Consider $\sigma$-finite measure spaces $(X, \mathcal{A}, \mu_1)$ and $(Y, \mathcal{B}, \mu_2)$. Let $\Omega = X \times Y$ and $\mathcal{F}$ be product $\sigma$-algebra. There is a unique measure $\mu$ on $\mathcal{F}$ with $\mu(A \times B) = \mu_1(A)\mu_2(B)$. If $f \ge 0$ or $\int |f| d\mu < \infty$ then $\int_{X} \int_{Y} f(x,y)\mu_{2}(dy)\mu_{1}(dx) = \int_{X\times Y} fd\mu = \int_{Y} \int_{X} f(x,y)\mu_{1}(dx)\mu_{2}(dy)$.
(Line 598)

## Statement 18
**Markov's inequality.** Let X be non-negative random variable. Fix $a > 0$. Then $P\{X \ge a\} \le \frac{E[X]}{a}$.
(Line 764)

## Statement 19
**Chebyshev's inequality.** If X has finite mean $\mu$, variance $\sigma^2$, and $k > 0$ then $P\{|X - \mu| \ge k\} \le \frac{\sigma^2}{k^2}$.
(Line 766)

## Statement 20
**Weak law of large numbers.** Suppose $X_i$ are i.i.d. random variables with mean $\mu$. Then the value $A_n := \frac{X_1 + X_2 + ... + X_n}{n}$ is called the empirical average of the first n trials. For all $\epsilon > 0$ we have $\lim_{n\to\infty} P\{|A_n - \mu| > \epsilon\} = 0$.
(Line 788)

## Statement 21
**Levy's continuity theorem.** If $\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{X_n}(t)=\phi_X(t)$ for all t, then $X_n$ converge in law to X.
(Line 857, repeated at 1252, 1600)

## Statement 22
**Lemma (First Borel-Cantelli).** If $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(A_n) < \infty$ then $P(A_n \text{ i.o.}) = 0$.
(Line 902)

## Statement 23
**Lemma (Second Borel-Cantelli).** If $A_n$ are independent, then $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(A_n) = \infty$ implies $P(A_n \text{ i.o.}) = 1$.
(Line 903)

## Statement 24
**Theorem (Convergence in probability and subsequential a.s. convergence).** $X_n \to X$ in probability if and only if for every subsequence of the $X_n$ there is a further subsequence converging a.s. to X.
(Line 907)

## Statement 25
**Theorem (Pairwise independence).** Suppose $A_1, A_2, ...$ are pairwise independent and $\sum P(A_n) = \infty$, and write $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n 1_{A_i}$. Then the ratio $S_n/ES_n$ tends a.s. to 1.

## Statement 26
**Theorem (Kolmogorov zero-one law).** If $X_1, X_2, ...$ are independent and $A \in \mathcal{T}$ (the tail $\sigma$-algebra) then $P(A) \in \{0, 1\}$.

## Statement 27
**Theorem (Kolmogorov maximal inequality).** Suppose $X_i$ are independent with mean zero and finite variances, and $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. Then $P(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|S_k|\geq x)\leq x^{-2}\mathrm{Var}(S_n)=x^{-2}E|S_n|^2$.
(Line 1089)

## Statement 28
**Theorem (Kolmogorov three-series theorem).** Let $X_1, X_2, ...$ be independent and fix $A > 0$. Write $Y_i = X_i 1_{(|X_i| \le A)}$. Then $\sum X_i$ converges a.s. if and only if the following are all true: (1) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} P(|X_n| > A) < \infty$, (2) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} EY_n$ converges, (3) $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \operatorname{Var}(Y_n) < \infty$.

## Statement 29
**Theorem (Stirling's formula).** $n! \sim n^n e^{-n} \sqrt{2\pi n}$ where $\sim$ means ratio tends to one.

## Statement 30

## Statement 31

## Statement 32

## Statement 34

## Statement 36

## Statement 37

## Statement 38
**Theorem (Strong continuity theorem).** If $\mu_n \implies \mu_\infty$ then $\phi_n(t) \to \phi_\infty(t)$ for all t. ## Statement 39
**Theorem (Central limit theorem).** Let $X_1, X_2, ...$ be i.i.d. with $EX_i = \mu$, $Var(X_i) = \sigma^2 \in (0, \infty)$. If $S_n = X_1 + ... + X_n$ then $(S_n - n\mu)/(\sigma n^{1/2})$ converges in law to a standard normal.

## Statement 40
**Theorem (Lindeberg-Feller).** Suppose $X_{n,m}$ are independent expectation-zero random variables when $1 \le m \le n$. Suppose $\sum_{m=1}^{n} EX_{n,m}^2 \to \sigma^2 > 0$ and for all $\epsilon$, $\lim_{n\to\infty} E(|X_{n,m}|^2; |X_{n,m}| > \epsilon) = 0$. Then $S_n = X_{n,1} + X_{n,2} + \ldots + X_{n,n} \implies \sigma \chi$ (where $\chi$ is standard normal) as $n \to \infty$.

## Statement 41
**Theorem (Berry-Esseen).** If $X_i$ are i.i.d. with mean zero, variance $\sigma^2$, and $E|X_i|^3 = \rho < \infty$, and $F_n(x)$ is distribution of $(X_1 + \ldots + X_n)/(\sigma\sqrt{n})$ and $\Phi(x)$ is standard normal distribution, then $|F_n(x) - \Phi(x)| \leq 3\rho/(\sigma^3\sqrt{n})$.

## Statement 42
**Theorem (Local limit theorem for lattice random walks).** Assume $X_i$ are i.i.d. lattice with $EX_i = 0$ and $EX_i^2 = \sigma^2 \in (0, \infty)$. ## Statement 43

## Statement 45

## Statement 56

## Statement 84
**Theorem (Martingale with bounded increments dichotomy).** A martingale with bounded increments a.s. either converges to a limit or oscillates between $\pm \infty$. That is, a.s. either $\lim X_n < \infty$ exists or $\limsup X_n = +\infty$ and $\liminf X_n = -\infty$.
