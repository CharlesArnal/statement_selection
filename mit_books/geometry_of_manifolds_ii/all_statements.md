# All Statements: Geometry of Manifolds II (Symplectic Geometry)

## Lecture 1

1. **Proposition 1** (L68): Cartan's magic formula: $L_X \alpha = di_X \alpha + i_X d\alpha$.

2. **Proposition 2** (L95): Poincare Lemma -- $H^p(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$ for all $p \geq 1$.

3. **Theorem 1** (L131): The de Rham and singular (simplicial) cohomologies are equivalent.

## Lecture 2

4. **Theorem 1** (L188): Every symplectic vector space $(V, \Omega)$ has a standard (symplectic) basis.

5. **Theorem 2** (L234): (Darboux) Every symplectic manifold is locally symplectomorphic to $(\mathbb{R}^{2n}, \omega_0)$.

## Lecture 3

6. **Proposition 1** (L283): A diffeomorphism $\phi$ is a symplectomorphism iff $\Gamma_\phi$ is a Lagrangian submanifold.

7. **Proposition 2** (L295): (Cartan's Formula, restated) $L_v \alpha = di_v \alpha + i_v d\alpha$. [Restatement of #1]

8. **Proposition 3** (L301): The flow $\rho_t$ of a Hamiltonian vector field consists of symplectomorphisms.

## Lecture 4

9. **Theorem 1** (L372): (Moser) If $(\omega_t)$ is a family of isotopic symplectic forms on a compact manifold M, then there exists an isotopy $\rho_t$ with $\rho_t^*\omega_t = \omega_0$.

10. **Theorem 2** (L385): (Darboux, restated) For $(M, \omega)$ symplectic, $p \in M$, there exist local Darboux coordinates $\omega|_U = \sum dx_i \wedge dy_i$. [Restatement of #5]

## Lecture 5

11. **Theorem 1** (L397): (Moser, restated) Compact manifold version. [Restatement of #9]

12. **Theorem 2** (L399): (Darboux, restated) Locally, any symplectic manifold is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{R}^{2n}, \omega_0)$. [Restatement of #5]

13. **Theorem 3** (L405): Tubular neighborhood theorem -- for $X^k \subset M^n$ a submanifold, there exist neighborhoods $U_0$ (of $X$ in $NX$) and $U_1$ (of $X$ in $M$) with a diffeomorphism $\phi: U_0 \to U_1$.

14. **Proposition 1** (L418): Relative Poincare lemma for submanifolds -- if $\beta$ is closed and $\beta|_X = 0$ on a tubular neighborhood, then $\beta = d\mu$ with $\mu|_X = 0$.

15. **Theorem 4** (L435): (Local Moser) If $\omega_0, \omega_1$ are symplectic forms agreeing on a submanifold $X$, then they are locally symplectomorphic near $X$.

16. **Proposition 2** (L444): If $X$ is a Lagrangian submanifold of $(M, \omega)$, then $NX \cong T^*X$.

17. **Theorem 5** (L448): (Weinstein's Lagrangian Neighborhood Theorem) A neighborhood of a closed Lagrangian submanifold $X$ in $(M, \omega)$ is symplectomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section in $(T^*X, \omega_0)$.

## Lecture 6

18. **Theorem 1** (L483): For $(M, \omega)$ compact with $H^1(M, \mathbb{R}) = 0$, every symplectomorphism $C^1$-close to the identity has $\geq 2$ fixed points.

19. **Theorem 2** (L485): For $X$ a compact Lagrangian with $H^1(X, \mathbb{R}) = 0$, every Lagrangian $C^1$-close to $X$ intersects $X$ in $\geq 2$ points.

## Lecture 7

20. **Theorem 1** (L526): (Arnold's conjecture) If fixed points of $f \in \text{Ham}(M, \omega)$ are nondegenerate, then $\#\text{Fix}(f) \geq \sum_i \dim H^i(M)$.

21. **Theorem 2** (L540): (Floer, Oh, Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono) Lagrangian intersection via Floer homology: $\#(L \cap \psi(L)) \geq \sum \dim H_i(L, \mathbb{Q})$ under suitable assumptions.

## Lecture 7 (continued)

22. **Proposition 1** (L557): For a symplectic vector space $(V, \Omega)$, there exists a compatible almost-complex structure $J$. Moreover, given any positive inner product, one can canonically construct such a $J$.

## Lecture 8

23. **Proposition 1** (L598): For $(M, \omega)$ a symplectic manifold with Riemannian metric $g$, there exists a canonical compatible almost complex structure $J$.

24. **Corollary 1** (L602): Any symplectic manifold has compatible almost-complex structures, and the space of such structures is path connected.

25. **Proposition 2** (L608): The set $\mathcal{J}(T_xM, \omega_x)$ of $\omega_x$-compatible complex structures on $T_xM$ is contractible.

26. **Proposition 3** (L618): For $(M, J)$ almost-complex and $\omega_0, \omega_1$ compatible symplectic forms, $\omega_t = (1-t)\omega_0 + t\omega_1$ is symplectic and $J$-compatible for all $t$.

27. **Proposition 4** (L633): If $X$ is an almost-complex submanifold in compatible $(M, \omega, J)$, then $X$ is symplectic.

28. **Proposition 5** (L644): $\mathrm{Sp}(2n) \cap O(2n) = \mathrm{Sp}(2n) \cap \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C}) = O(2n) \cap \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C}) = U(n)$.

## Lecture 9

29. **Proposition 1** (L694): The curvature $R^\nabla$ is a tensor (depends only on pointwise values, not derivatives of sections).

30. **Proposition 2** (L719): Under a change of trivialization by $g$, $R^\nabla \mapsto g^{-1} R^\nabla g$, i.e., $R^\nabla = dA + A \wedge A$ is well-defined in $\Omega^2(M, \mathrm{End}\, E)$.

## Lecture 10

31. **Proposition 1** (L756): $R^\nabla = (d^\nabla)^2$, i.e., the curvature is the square of the covariant exterior derivative.

32. **Theorem 1** (L795): The Chern forms $c_j(E, \nabla)$ are closed, and the Chern classes $c_j(E) = [c_j(E, \nabla)] \in H^{2j}(M, \mathbb{R})$ are independent of the connection.

## Lecture 11

33. **Proposition 1** (L813): For $M$ compact and oriented, the top Chern class equals the Euler class: $c_r(E) = e(E)$.

## Lecture 12

34. **Theorem 1** (L889): (Hirzebruch signature theorem for 4-manifolds) $p_1(TM) \cdot [M] = 3\sigma(M)$.

35. **Theorem 2** (L898): (Almost complex structure existence criterion for 4-manifolds) An almost complex structure $J$ on $M^4$ with $c_1(TM, J) = \alpha$ exists iff $\alpha^2 \cdot [M] = 2\chi + 3\sigma$ and $\alpha \cdot [A] \equiv Q(A, A) \mod 2$ for all $[A] \in H_2$.

## Lecture 13

36. **Proposition 1** (L987): $N(u, v) = -8\mathrm{Re}([u^{1,0}, v^{1,0}]^{0,1})$.

37. **Corollary 1** (L991): $N = 0$ iff $[T^{1,0}, T^{1,0}] \subset T^{1,0}$.

38. **Proposition 2** (L993): The Nijenhuis tensor $N$ is a tensor (depends only on pointwise values).

39. **Proposition 3** (L999): If $J$ is a complex structure (integrable), then $N = 0$.

40. **Theorem 1** (L1003): (Newlander-Nirenberg) $N \equiv 0$ iff $J$ is integrable.

41. **Proposition 4** (L1011): The dual Nijenhuis tensor $N^*: \bigwedge^{0,1} T^*M \to \bigwedge^{2,0} T^*M$ maps $\alpha \mapsto (d\alpha)^{(2,0)}$.

42. **Theorem 2** (L1027): (Newlander-Nirenberg, equivalent characterizations) $J$ integrable $\Leftrightarrow N \equiv 0 \Leftrightarrow [T^{1,0}, T^{1,0}] \subset T^{1,0} \Leftrightarrow d = \partial + \overline{\partial} \Leftrightarrow \overline{\partial}^2 = 0$. [Expanded restatement of #40]

## Lecture 14

43. **Proposition 1** (L1084): $\phi$ is strictly plurisubharmonic iff $\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}\phi$ is Kahler.

44. **Theorem 1** (L1090): (Local Kahler potential / $\partial\overline{\partial}$-lemma) For $\omega$ a closed real (1,1)-form, locally $\omega = \frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}\phi$.

45. **Theorem 2** (L1128): (Kodaira Embedding) A compact Kahler manifold $(X, \omega, J)$ with $[\omega]$ integral embeds holomorphically in $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

46. **Theorem 3** (L1130): (Hodge Decomposition) For $(M, \omega)$ compact Kahler, $H^k(M, \mathbb{C}) = \bigoplus_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(M)$ and $H^{p,q} \cong \overline{H^{q,p}}$.
