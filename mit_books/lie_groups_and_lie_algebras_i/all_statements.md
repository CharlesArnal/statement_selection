# All Mathematical Statements from Lie Groups and Lie Algebras I

## Statement 1: Lemma 1.4
If X is a topological space with a countable base then every open cover of X has a countable subcover.

## Statement 2: Lemma 1.14
Let $x_1, ..., x_n$ be local coordinates at P. Then $T_PX$ has basis $D_1, ..., D_n$, where $D_i(f) := \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(0)$.

## Statement 3: Proposition 1.18
If F is a submersion then for any $Q \in Y$, $F^{-1}(Q)$ is a manifold of dimension $\dim X - \dim Y$.

## Statement 4: Proposition 2.2
In a Lie group G, the inversion map $\iota: G \to G$ is a diffeomorphism, and $d\iota_1 = -\mathrm{Id}$.

## Statement 5: Proposition 2.6
(i) $G^{\circ}$ is a normal subgroup of G. (ii) $\pi_0(G) = G/G^{\circ}$ with quotient topology is a discrete and countable group.

## Statement 6: Proposition 3.5
(i) $\widetilde{G}$ is a simply connected Lie group. The covering $p: \widetilde{G} \to G$ is a homomorphism of Lie groups. (ii) $\operatorname{Ker}(p)$ is a central subgroup of $\widetilde{G}$ naturally isomorphic to $\pi_1(G)$. Thus, $\widetilde{G}$ is a central extension of G by $\pi_1(G)$. In particular, $\pi_1(G)$ is abelian.

## Statement 7: Lemma 3.11
A closed Lie subgroup of G is closed in G.

## Statement 8: Theorem 3.13
Any closed subgroup of a real Lie group G is a closed Lie subgroup.

## Statement 9: Proposition 3.15
(i) If G is a connected Lie group and U a neighborhood of 1 in G then U generates G. (ii) G is generated by $\exp(\mathfrak{g})$.

## Statement 10: Theorem 4.1
(i) Let G be a Lie group of dimension n and $H \subset G$ a closed Lie subgroup of dimension k. Then the homogeneous space G/H has a natural structure of an n-k-dimensional manifold, and the map $p: G \to G/H$ is a locally trivial fibration with fiber H. (ii) If H is normal then G/H is a Lie group.

## Statement 11: Proposition 4.7
Let $f: G \to K$ be a homomorphism of Lie groups. Then $H := \operatorname{Ker} f$ is a closed normal Lie subgroup in G and $\operatorname{Im} f$ is a Lie subgroup in K, closed if and only if it is an embedded submanifold. In the latter case, we have an isomorphism of Lie groups $G/H \cong \operatorname{Im} f$.

## Statement 12: Proposition 4.12
(The orbit-stabilizer theorem for Lie group actions) The stabilizer $G_x \subset G$ is a closed Lie subgroup, and the natural map $G/G_x \to X$ is an injective immersion whose image is Gx.

## Statement 13: Corollary 4.13
The orbit $Gx \subset X$ is an immersed submanifold, and we have a natural isomorphism $T_x(Gx) \cong T_1G/T_1G_x$. If Gx is an embedded submanifold then the map $G/G_x \to Gx$ is a diffeomorphism.

## Statement 14: Corollary 4.16
If G acts transitively on X then the map $p: G \to X$ given by p(g) = gx is a locally trivial fibration with fiber $G_x$.

## Statement 15: Proposition 5.10
(i) For any $\tau \in \mathfrak{g}^{\otimes k} \otimes \mathfrak{g}^{*\otimes m}$ there exists a unique left invariant tensor field $\mathbf{L}_{\tau}$ and a unique right invariant tensor field $\mathbf{R}_{\tau}$ whose value at 1 is $\tau$. Thus, the spaces of such tensor fields are naturally isomorphic to $\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes k} \otimes \mathfrak{g}^{*\otimes m}$.

## Statement 16: Proposition 6.3
Every classical group G from the above list is a Lie group, with $\mathfrak{g} = T_1G \subset \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{K})$. Moreover, if $\mathfrak{u} \subset \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{K})$ is a small neighborhood of 0 and $U = \exp(\mathfrak{u})$ then $\exp$ and $\log$ define mutually inverse diffeomorphisms between $\mathfrak{u} \cap \mathfrak{g}$ and $U \cap G$.

## Statement 17: Proposition 6.7
The group of unit quaternions $\{\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{H} : |\mathbf{q}| = 1\}$ under multiplication is isomorphic to SU(2) as a Lie group.

## Statement 18: Lemma 6.10
We have $\det A > 0$.

## Statement 19: Proposition 6.13
(i) Every nondegenerate Hermitian form on V in some basis takes the form $\sum_{i=1}^{p} |z_i|^2 - \sum_{i=p+1}^{n} |z_i|^2$ for some p (the signature). (ii) Two nondegenerate Hermitian forms are equivalent iff they have the same signature.

## Statement 20: Proposition 7.1
Let $x \in \mathfrak{g}$. There is a unique morphism of Lie groups $\gamma = \gamma_x : \mathbb{R} \to G$ such that $\gamma'(0) = x$.

## Statement 21: Proposition 7.3
The flow defined by the right-invariant vector field $\mathbf{R}_x$ is given by $g \mapsto \exp(tx)g$, and the flow defined by the left-invariant vector field $\mathbf{L}_x$ is given by $g \mapsto g \exp(tx)$.

## Statement 22: Theorem 7.5
(i) $\exp : \mathfrak{g} \to G$ is a regular map which is a diffeomorphism of a neighborhood of $0 \in \mathfrak{g}$ onto a neighborhood of $1 \in G$, with $\exp(0) = 1$, $\exp'(0) = \operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}$. (ii) $\exp((s+t)x) = \exp(sx) \exp(tx)$ for $x \in \mathfrak{g}$, $s, t \in \mathbb{K}$. (iii) For any morphism of Lie groups $\phi: G \to K$ and $x \in T_1G$ we have $\phi(\exp(x)) = \exp(\phi_* x)$. (iv) For any $g \in G$, $x \in \mathfrak{g}$, we have $g \exp(x)g^{-1} = \exp(\mathrm{Ad}_g x)$.

## Statement 23: Proposition 7.6
Let G be a connected Lie group and $\phi: G \to K$ a morphism of Lie groups. Then $\phi$ is completely determined by the linear map $\phi_*: T_1G \to T_1K$.

## Statement 24: Proposition 7.11
(i) Let G, K be Lie groups and $\phi: G \to K$ a morphism of Lie groups. Then $\phi_*: T_1G \to T_1K$ preserves the commutator: $\phi_*([x,y]) = [\phi_*(x), \phi_*(y)]$. (ii) The adjoint action preserves the commutator. (iii) $\exp(x) \exp(y) \exp(x)^{-1} \exp(y)^{-1} = \exp([x, y] + ...)$ where ... denotes cubic and higher terms. (iv) $[x,y] = \lim_{s,t\to 0} \frac{\log(X(t)Y(s)X(t)^{-1}Y(s)^{-1})}{ts}$. (v) If G is commutative then [x, y] = 0 for all x, y.

## Statement 25: Proposition 8.1
The Jacobi identity holds for any Lie group G.

## Statement 26: Proposition 8.3
For $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ one has $\exp(\operatorname{ad} x) = \operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(x)} \in GL(\mathfrak{g})$.

## Statement 27: Theorem 8.8
If G is a $\mathbb{K}$-Lie group (for $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}$) then $\mathfrak{g} := T_1G$ has a natural structure of a Lie algebra over $\mathbb{K}$. Moreover, if $\phi : G \to K$ is a morphism of Lie groups then $\phi_* : T_1G \to T_1K$ is a morphism of Lie algebras.

## Statement 28: Proposition 8.9
Let $H \subset G$ be a Lie subgroup. Then: (i) $\mathfrak{h} = \text{Lie } H$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g} = \text{Lie } G$. (ii) If H is normal then $\mathfrak{h}$ is an ideal. (iii) $\text{Lie}(G/H) \cong \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}$ when H is closed and normal.

## Statement 29: Proposition 8.10
If $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}$ are derivations of an algebra A then so is $[\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}] := \mathbf{v}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{w}\mathbf{v}$.

## Statement 30: Proposition 8.12
$\operatorname{Vect}_L(G)$, $\operatorname{Vect}_R(G) \subset \operatorname{Vect}(G)$ are Lie subalgebras which are both canonically isomorphic to $\mathfrak{g} = \operatorname{Lie} G$.

## Statement 31: Proposition 9.1
The map $a_*$ is linear and we have $a_*(x)_P = \frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0} \exp(tx) \cdot P$.

## Statement 32: Theorem 9.4
(i) The stabilizer $G_x$ is a closed subgroup of G with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_x := \{z \in \mathfrak{g} : a_*(z)_x = 0\}$. (ii) The orbit map $G/G_x \to Gx$ is an injective immersion, and $T_x(Gx) \cong \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_x$.

## Statement 33: Corollary 9.6
Let V be a finite dimensional representation of a Lie group G, and $v \in V$. Then the stabilizer $G_v$ is a closed Lie subgroup of G with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_v := \{z \in \mathfrak{g} : zv = 0\}$.

## Statement 34: Proposition 9.8
If G is connected then Z is a closed (normal, commutative) Lie subgroup of G with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{z}$.

## Statement 35: Theorem 9.11
(First fundamental theorem of Lie theory) For a Lie group G, there is a bijection between connected Lie subgroups $H \subset G$ and Lie subalgebras $\mathfrak{h} \subset \mathfrak{g} = \mathrm{Lie} G$, given by $\mathfrak{h} = \mathrm{Lie} H$.

## Statement 36: Theorem 9.12
(Second fundamental theorem of Lie theory) If G and K are Lie groups with G simply connected then the map $\phi \mapsto \phi_*$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}(G, K) \to \operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{k})$.

## Statement 37: Theorem 9.13
(Third fundamental theorem of Lie theory) Any finite dimensional Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of a Lie group.

## Statement 38: Theorem 10.4
(The Frobenius theorem) A distribution D is integrable if and only if for every two vector fields $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}$ contained in D, their commutator $[\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}]$ is also contained in D.

## Statement 39: Theorem 10.6
Any finite dimensional Lie algebra over $\mathbb{K}$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{K})$.

## Statement 40: Theorem 10.7
Any finite dimensional $\mathbb{K}$-Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of a Lie subgroup of $GL_n(\mathbb{K})$ for some n.

## Statement 41: Lemma 11.9
(Schur's lemma) Let V, W be irreducible finite dimensional complex representations of G or $\mathfrak{g}$. Then $\text{Hom}_{G,\mathfrak{g}}(V,W)=0$ if V,W are not isomorphic, and every endomorphism of the representation V is a scalar.

## Statement 42: Corollary 11.10
The center of G, $\mathfrak{g}$ acts on an irreducible representation by a scalar. In particular, if G or $\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian then every irreducible representation of G is 1-dimensional.

## Statement 43: Proposition 11.13
Any unitary representation can be written as an orthogonal direct sum of irreducible unitary representations. In particular, it is completely reducible.

## Statement 44: Proposition 11.14
Any finite dimensional representation V of a finite group G is unitary. Moreover, if V is irreducible, the unitary structure is unique up to a positive factor.

## Statement 45: Proposition 12.2
(i) Let $J \subset T\mathfrak{g}$ be an ideal, and $\rho : \mathfrak{g} \to T\mathfrak{g}/J$ the natural linear map. Then $\rho$ is a homomorphism of Lie algebras if and only if $J \supset I$, so that $T\mathfrak{g}/J$ is a quotient of $T\mathfrak{g}/I = U(\mathfrak{g})$. In other words, $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is the largest quotient of $T\mathfrak{g}$ for which $\rho$ is a homomorphism of Lie algebras.

## Statement 46: Proposition 12.4
The center $Z(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ of $U(\mathfrak{g})$ coincides with the subalgebra of invariants $U(\mathfrak{g})^{ad\mathfrak{g}}$.

## Statement 47: Proposition 12.6
If gr(A) is a domain (has no zero divisors) then so is A.

## Statement 48: Lemma 12.9
If $\mathfrak{g}$ is a Lie algebra then the kernel I of the map $T\mathfrak{g} \to U(\mathfrak{g})$ satisfies the property $\Delta(I) \subset I \otimes T\mathfrak{g} + T\mathfrak{g} \otimes I \subset T\mathfrak{g} \otimes T\mathfrak{g}$. Thus $\Delta$ descends to an algebra homomorphism $U(\mathfrak{g}) \to U(\mathfrak{g}) \otimes U(\mathfrak{g})$.

## Statement 49: Theorem 13.1
(Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt theorem) The homomorphism $\phi: S\mathfrak{g} \to \operatorname{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ is an isomorphism.

## Statement 50: Theorem 13.2
The ordered monomials are linearly independent, hence form a basis of $U(\mathfrak{g})$.

## Statement 51: Lemma 13.11
There exists a unique linear map $\varphi : T\mathfrak{g} \to S\mathfrak{g}$ such that $\varphi(x_1 \cdots x_n) = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} x_{\sigma(1)} \cdots x_{\sigma(n)}$ and $\varphi$ descends to a linear isomorphism $U(\mathfrak{g}) \to S\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 52: Lemma 14.1
If the ground field $\mathbf{k}$ has characteristic zero then every primitive element of $U(\mathfrak{g})$ is contained in $\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 53: Proposition 14.4
(i) $\psi$ is an isomorphism, so $U(L(V)) \cong TV$. (ii) $L(V)$ is a free Lie algebra on V.

## Statement 54: Theorem 14.6
For each $n \geq 1$, $\mu_n(x,y)$ may be written as a $\mathbb{Q}$-Lie polynomial of x, y (i.e., a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of Lie monomials, obtained by taking successive commutators of x, y), which is universal (i.e., independent on G).

## Statement 55: Lemma 15.1
If $I_1, I_2 \subset \mathfrak{g}$ are ideals then so are $I_1 \cap I_2, I_1 + I_2$ and $[I_1, I_2]$.

## Statement 56: Lemma 15.4
The quotient $\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ is abelian; moreover, if $I \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is an ideal such that $\mathfrak{g}/I$ is abelian then $I \supset [\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$.

## Statement 57: Proposition 15.9
The following conditions on $\mathfrak{g}$ are equivalent: (i) $\mathfrak{g}$ is solvable; (ii) $\mathfrak{g}^{(n)} = 0$ for some n; (iii) there exists a chain of ideals $0 = I_0 \subset I_1 \subset ... \subset I_n = \mathfrak{g}$ such that $I_{k+1}/I_k$ is abelian for all k.

## Statement 58: Proposition 15.10
(i) Any Lie subalgebra or quotient of a solvable Lie algebra is solvable. (ii) If $I \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is a solvable ideal and $\mathfrak{g}/I$ is solvable then $\mathfrak{g}$ is solvable.

## Statement 59: Proposition 15.13
The following conditions on $\mathfrak{g}$ are equivalent: (i) $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent; (ii) $\mathfrak{g}^n = 0$ for some n; (iii) there is a chain of ideals $0 = I_0 \subset ... \subset I_n = \mathfrak{g}$ such that $[I_{k+1}, \mathfrak{g}] \subset I_k$ for all k.

## Statement 60: Proposition 15.15
Any Lie subalgebra or quotient of a nilpotent Lie algebra is nilpotent.

## Statement 61: Lemma 15.18
Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathbf{k}x \oplus \mathfrak{h}$ be a Lie algebra over a field $\mathbf{k}$ in which $\mathfrak{h}$ is an ideal. Let V be a finite dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module and $v \in V$ a common eigenvector of $\mathfrak{h}$: $av = \lambda(a)v$ for $a \in \mathfrak{h}$, where $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$. Then: (i) The span of $x^n v$ for $n \geq 0$ is a $\mathfrak{g}$-submodule W, and any $a \in \mathfrak{h}$ on W has the only eigenvalue $\lambda(a)$. (ii) $\lambda([x,a]) = 0$ for any $a \in \mathfrak{h}$, i.e. $\lambda|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}]} = 0$.

## Statement 62: Theorem 15.19
(Lie's theorem) Let $\mathbf{k}$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and $\mathfrak{g}$ a finite dimensional solvable Lie algebra over $\mathbf{k}$. Then any irreducible finite dimensional representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is 1-dimensional.

## Statement 63: Theorem 15.24
Let $V \neq 0$ be a finite dimensional vector space over any field $\mathbf{k}$, and $\mathfrak{g} \subset \mathfrak{gl}(V)$ be a Lie algebra consisting of nilpotent operators. Then there exists a nonzero vector $v \in V$ such that $\mathfrak{g}v = 0$.

## Statement 64: Corollary 15.26
(Engel's theorem) A finite dimensional Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent if and only if every element $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent.

## Statement 65: Proposition 16.1
The sum of all solvable ideals of $\mathfrak{g}$ is a solvable ideal.

## Statement 66: Proposition 16.4
(i) We have $rad(\mathfrak{g} \oplus \mathfrak{h}) = rad(\mathfrak{g}) \oplus rad(\mathfrak{h})$. In particular, the direct sum of semisimple Lie algebras is semisimple. (ii) Any quotient of a reductive Lie algebra is reductive.

## Statement 67: Theorem 16.6
(Weak Levi decomposition) The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{ss} = \mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g})$ is semisimple. Thus any $\mathfrak{g}$ can be included in an exact sequence $0 \to \mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g}) \to \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}_{ss} \to 0$ where $\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g})$ is a solvable ideal and $\mathfrak{g}_{ss}$ is semisimple. Moreover, if $\mathfrak{h} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is a solvable ideal such that $\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}$ is semisimple then $\mathfrak{h} = \mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{g})$.

## Statement 68: Theorem 16.7
(Levi decomposition) If $\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k}) = 0$ then we have $\mathfrak{g} \cong \operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{g}) \oplus \mathfrak{g}_{ss}$ as vector spaces, where $\mathfrak{g}_{ss} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is a semisimple subalgebra; i.e., $\mathfrak{g}$ is isomorphic to the semidirect product $\mathfrak{g}_{ss} \ltimes \operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{g})$.

## Statement 69: Proposition 16.9
Let $\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k}) = 0$, $\mathbf{k}$ algebraically closed, and V be an irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{g}$. Then $\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{g})$ acts on V by scalars, and $[\mathfrak{g}, \operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{g})]$ by zero.

## Statement 70: Proposition 16.12
If B is a symmetric invariant bilinear form on $\mathfrak{g}$ and $I \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is an ideal then the orthogonal complement $I^{\perp} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is also an ideal. In particular, $\mathfrak{g}^{\perp} = \operatorname{Ker}(B)$ is an ideal in $\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 71: Proposition 16.14
If $B_V$ is nondegenerate for some V then $\mathfrak{g}$ is reductive.

## Statement 72: Proposition 16.16
All classical Lie algebras over $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are reductive.

## Statement 73: Theorem 16.18
(Cartan criterion of solvability) A Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field $\mathbf{k}$ of characteristic zero is solvable if and only if $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}] \subset \mathrm{Ker}(K)$.

## Statement 74: Theorem 16.19
(Cartan criterion of semisimplicity) A Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field $\mathbf{k}$ of characteristic zero is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is nondegenerate.

## Statement 75: Proposition 16.21
A square matrix $A \in \mathfrak{gl}_N(\mathbf{k})$ over a field $\mathbf{k}$ of characteristic zero can be uniquely written as $A_s + A_n$, where $A_s$ is semisimple and $A_n$ is nilpotent in such a way that $A_sA_n = A_nA_s$. Moreover, $A_s = P(A)$ for some $P \in \mathbf{k}[x]$.

## Statement 76: Lemma 17.1
Let $\mathfrak{g} \subset \mathfrak{gl}(V)$ be a Lie subalgebra such that for any $x \in [\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ and $y \in \mathfrak{g}$ we have $\operatorname{Tr}(xy) = 0$. Then $\mathfrak{g}$ is solvable.

## Statement 77: Proposition 17.2
Let $\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k}) = 0$ and $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite dimensional Lie algebra over $\mathbf{k}$. Then $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple iff $\mathfrak{g} \otimes_{\mathbf{k}} \overline{\mathbf{k}}$ is semisimple.

## Statement 78: Theorem 17.4
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra and $I \subset \mathfrak{g}$ an ideal. Then there is an ideal $J \subset \mathfrak{g}$ such that $\mathfrak{g} = I \oplus J$.

## Statement 79: Corollary 17.5
A Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple iff it is a direct sum of simple Lie algebras.

## Statement 80: Corollary 17.6
If $\mathfrak{g}$ is a semisimple Lie algebra, then $[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}] = \mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 81: Proposition 17.7
Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_1 \oplus ... \oplus \mathfrak{g}_k$ be a semisimple Lie algebra, with $\mathfrak{g}_i$ being simple. Then any ideal I in $\mathfrak{g}$ is of the form $I = \bigoplus_{i \in S} \mathfrak{g}_i$ for some subset $S \subset \{1, ..., k\}$.

## Statement 82: Corollary 17.8
Any ideal in a semisimple Lie algebra is semisimple. Also, any quotient of a semisimple Lie algebra is semisimple.

## Statement 83: Proposition 17.9
If $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple then $\mathfrak{g} = \operatorname{Der} \mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 84: Lemma 18.2
A short exact sequence $0 \to U \to V \to W \to 0$ gives rise to an exact sequence $H^1(\mathfrak{g},U) \to H^1(\mathfrak{g},V) \to H^1(\mathfrak{g},W)$.

## Statement 85: Theorem 18.4
(Whitehead) If $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple in characteristic zero then for every finite dimensional representation V of $\mathfrak{g}$, $H^1(\mathfrak{g}, V) = 0$.

## Statement 86: Lemma 18.5
Let E be a representation of a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ and $C \in U(\mathfrak{g})$ be a central element which acts by 0 on the trivial representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ and by some scalar $\lambda \neq 0$ on E. Then $H^1(\mathfrak{g}, E) = 0$.

## Statement 87: Lemma 18.6
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be semisimple in characteristic zero and V be a nontrivial finite dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{g}$-module. Then there is a central element $C \in U(\mathfrak{g})$ such that $C|_{\mathbf{k}} = 0$ and $C|_{V} \neq 0$.

## Statement 88: Corollary 18.7
For any irreducible finite dimensional representation V of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field $\mathbf{k}$ of characteristic zero, we have $H^1(\mathfrak{g}, V) = 0$.

## Statement 89: Corollary 18.8
A reductive Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ in characteristic zero is uniquely a direct sum of a semisimple and abelian Lie algebra.

## Statement 90: Theorem 18.9
Every finite dimensional representation of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field of characteristic zero is completely reducible, i.e., isomorphic to a direct sum of irreducible representations.

## Statement 91: Lemma 19.1
We have $[\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda},\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}] \subset \mathfrak{g}_{\lambda+\mu}$.

## Statement 92: Proposition 19.3
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero. Then every element $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ has a unique decomposition as $x = x_s + x_n$, where $x_s$ is semisimple, $x_n$ is nilpotent and $[x_s, x_n] = 0$. Moreover, if $y \in \mathfrak{g}$ and [x, y] = 0 then $[x_s, y] = [x_n, y] = 0$.

## Statement 93: Corollary 19.4
Any semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} \neq 0$ over a field of characteristic zero contains nonzero semisimple elements.

## Statement 94: Proposition 19.6
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra, $\mathfrak{h} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ a toral subalgebra, and B a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form on $\mathfrak{g}$. Then: (i) We have a decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \mathfrak{h}^*} \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$, where $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$ is the $\alpha$-weight space, and $\mathfrak{g}_0 \supset \mathfrak{h}$. (ii) $[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}] \subset \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}$. (iii) If $\alpha + \beta \neq 0$ then $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$ and $\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}$ are orthogonal under B. (iv) B restricts to a nondegenerate pairing $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha} \times \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha} \to \mathbf{k}$.

## Statement 95: Corollary 19.7
(i) The Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_0 \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is reductive. (ii) If $x \in \mathfrak{g}_0$ then $x_s, x_n \in \mathfrak{g}_0$.

## Statement 96: Theorem 19.10
Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be a maximal toral subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$. Then $\mathfrak{h}$ is a Cartan subalgebra.

## Statement 97: Proposition 19.11
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra, $\mathfrak{h} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ a Cartan subalgebra, and B a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form on $\mathfrak{g}$. Then: (i) We have a decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in R} \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$, where R is the (finite) set of nonzero $\alpha \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ such that $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha} \neq 0$. (ii) $[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}] \subset \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}$. (iii) If $\alpha + \beta \neq 0$ then $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$ and $\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}$ are orthogonal under B. (iv) B restricts to a nondegenerate pairing $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha} \times \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha} \to \mathbf{k}$.

## Statement 98: Proposition 19.13
Let $\mathfrak{g}_1,...,\mathfrak{g}_n$ be simple Lie algebras and let $\mathfrak{g} = \bigoplus_i \mathfrak{g}_i$. (i) The Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ is $\mathfrak{h} = \bigoplus_i \mathfrak{h}_i$ and the root system R is the disjoint union of $R_i$. (ii) Each Cartan subalgebra in $\mathfrak{g}$ has the form $\mathfrak{h} = \bigoplus_i \mathfrak{h}_i$ where $\mathfrak{h}_i \subset \mathfrak{g}_i$ is a Cartan subalgebra.

## Statement 99: Lemma 19.15
For any $e \in \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$, $f \in \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}$ we have $[e, f] = (e, f)H_{\alpha}$.

## Statement 100: Lemma 19.16
(i) If $\alpha$ is a root then $(\alpha, \alpha) \neq 0$. (ii) Let $e \in \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$, $f \in \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}$ be such that $(e, f) = \frac{2}{(\alpha, \alpha)}$, and let $h_{\alpha} := \frac{2H_{\alpha}}{(\alpha, \alpha)}$. Then $e, f, h_{\alpha}$ satisfy the commutation relations of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. (iii) $h_{\alpha}$ is independent on the choice of the inner product.

## Statement 101: Proposition 19.17
Let $\mathfrak{a}_{\alpha} = \mathbf{k} H_{\alpha} \oplus \bigoplus_{k \neq 0} \mathfrak{g}_{k\alpha} \subset \mathfrak{g}$. Then $\mathfrak{a}_{\alpha}$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 102: Corollary 19.18
(i) The space $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$ is 1-dimensional for each root $\alpha$ of $\mathfrak{g}$. (ii) If $\alpha$ is a root of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $k \geq 2$ is an integer then $k\alpha$ is not a root of $\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 103: Theorem 19.19
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ and root decomposition. Let (,) be a non-degenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form. Then: (i) R spans $\mathfrak{h}^*$ and elements $h_{\alpha}$ span $\mathfrak{h}$. (ii) For any two roots $\alpha, \beta$, $a_{\alpha,\beta} := \beta(h_{\alpha}) = \frac{2(\alpha,\beta)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}$ is an integer. (iii) For any roots $\alpha, \beta$, $s_{\alpha}(\beta)$ is also a root. (iv) For roots $\alpha, \beta \neq \pm \alpha$, the subspace $V_{\alpha,\beta} = \bigoplus_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathfrak{g}_{\beta+k\alpha}$ is an irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbf{k})_{\alpha}$.

## Statement 104: Corollary 19.20
Let $\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}$ be the $\mathbb{R}$-span of all $h_{\alpha}$. Then $\mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}} \oplus i\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}$ and the restriction of the Killing form to $\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}$ is real-valued and positive definite.

## Statement 105: Lemma 20.4
Let $P(z_1,...,z_n)$ be a nonzero complex polynomial, and $U \subset \mathbb{C}^n$ be the set of points such that $P(z_1,...,z_n) \neq 0$. Then U is path-connected, dense and open.

## Statement 106: Lemma 20.5
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex semisimple Lie algebra. Then the set $\mathfrak{g}^{sr}$ of strongly regular elements is connected, dense and open in $\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 107: Proposition 20.6
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and $\mathfrak{h} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ a Cartan subalgebra. Then (i) dim $\mathfrak{h} = \operatorname{rank}(\mathfrak{g})$; and (ii) the set $\mathfrak{h}^{\rm reg}:=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\rm sr}$ coincides with $\{ h \in \mathfrak{h} : \alpha(h) \neq 0 \ \forall \alpha \in R \}$.

## Statement 108: Lemma 20.7
Let $\phi: G \times \mathfrak{h} \to \mathfrak{g}$ be the map defined by $\phi(g,x) := \operatorname{Ad} g \cdot x$. Then the set $U := \phi(G \times V) \subset \mathfrak{g}$ is open.

## Statement 109: Theorem 20.8
(i) Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and let $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ be a strongly regular semisimple element. Then the centralizer C(x) of x in $\mathfrak{g}$ is a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$. (ii) Any Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ is of this form.

## Statement 110: Corollary 20.9
(i) Any strongly regular element $x \in \mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple. (ii) Such x is contained in a unique Cartan subalgebra, namely $\mathfrak{h}_x = C(x)$.

## Statement 111: Theorem 20.10
Any two Cartan subalgebras of a complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ are conjugate. I.e., if $\mathfrak{h}_1, \mathfrak{h}_2 \subset \mathfrak{g}$ are two Cartan subalgebras and G a connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ then there exists an element $g \in G$ such that $\mathrm{Ad}\, g \cdot \mathfrak{h}_1 = \mathfrak{h}_2$.

## Statement 112: Proposition 21.3
If $\mathfrak{g}$ is a semisimple Lie algebra and $\mathfrak{h} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ a Cartan subalgebra then the corresponding set of roots R is a reduced root system, and $\alpha^{\vee} = h_{\alpha}$.

## Statement 113: Proposition 21.7
W is a finite subgroup of O(E) which preserves R.

## Statement 114: Theorem 21.9
Let R be a reduced root system and $\alpha, \beta \in R$ be two linearly independent roots with $|\alpha| \geq |\beta|$. Let $\phi$ be the angle between $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then $n_{\alpha\beta}n_{\beta\alpha} = 4\cos^2\phi \in \{0,1,2,3\}$, and the possible cases are enumerated (7 cases with specific angles and length ratios).

## Statement 115: Theorem 21.10
Any reduced rank 2 root system R is of the form $A_1 \times A_1$, $A_2$, $B_2$ or $G_2$.

## Statement 116: Corollary 21.11
If $\alpha, \beta \in R$ are independent roots with $(\alpha, \beta) < 0$ then $\alpha + \beta \in R$.

## Statement 117: Lemma 21.14
Every positive root is a sum of simple roots.

## Statement 118: Lemma 21.15
If $\alpha, \beta \in R_+$ are simple roots then $(\alpha, \beta) \leq 0$.

## Statement 119: Theorem 21.16
The set $\Pi \subset R_+$ of simple roots is a basis of E.

## Statement 120: Lemma 21.17
Let $v_i$ be vectors in a Euclidean space E such that $(v_i, v_j) \leq 0$ when $i \neq j$ and $t(v_i) > 0$ for some $t \in E^*$. Then $v_i$ are linearly independent.

## Statement 121: Corollary 21.19
Any root $\alpha \in R$ can be uniquely written as $\alpha = \sum_{i=1}^{r} n_i \alpha_i$, where $n_i \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $\alpha$ is positive then $n_i \geq 0$ for all i and if $\alpha$ is negative then $n_i \leq 0$ for all i.

## Statement 122: Lemma 22.2
(i) The closure $\overline{C}$ of a Weyl chamber C is a convex cone. (ii) The boundary of $\overline{C}$ is a union of codimension 1 faces which are convex cones inside one of the root hyperplanes.

## Statement 123: Lemma 22.3
The assignments (polarization to Weyl chamber, and vice versa) are mutually inverse bijections between polarizations of R and Weyl chambers.

## Statement 124: Theorem 22.5

Any two polarizations of R are related by the action of an element $w \in W$. Thus if $\Pi, \Pi'$ are systems of simple roots corresponding to two polarizations then there is $w \in W$ such that $w(\Pi) = \Pi'$.

## Statement 127: Lemma 22.8
For every Weyl chamber C there exist $i_1, ..., i_m$ such that $C = s_{i_1}...s_{i_m}(C_+)$.

## Statement 128: Corollary 22.9
(i) The simple reflections $s_i$ generate W; (ii) $W(\Pi) = R$.

## Statement 129: Proposition 22.13
Let $\rho = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\alpha \in R_+} \alpha$. Then $(\rho, \alpha_i^{\vee}) = 1$ for all i. Thus $\rho = \sum_{i=1}^r \omega_i$.

## Statement 130: Theorem 22.14
Let $w = s_{i_1}...s_{i_l}$ be a representation of $w \in W$ as a product of simple reflections that has minimal possible length. Then $l = \ell(w)$.

## Statement 131: Proposition 22.15
The Weyl group W acts simply transitively on Weyl chambers.

## Statement 132: Proposition 22.16
$E/W = \overline{C}_+$, i.e., every W-orbit on E has a unique representative in $\overline{C}_+$.

## Statement 133: Corollary 22.17
Let $C_- = -C_+$ be the negative Weyl chamber. Then there exists a unique $w_0 \in W$ such that $w_0(C_+) = C_-$. We have $\ell(w_0) = |R_+|$ and for any $w \neq w_0$, $\ell(w) < \ell(w_0)$. Also $w_0^2 = 1$.

## Statement 134: Lemma 23.2
If R is a root system with system of simple roots $\Pi = \Pi_1 \sqcup \Pi_2$ with $\Pi_1 \perp \Pi_2$ then $R = R_1 \sqcup R_2$ where $R_i$ is the root system generated by $\Pi_i$.

## Statement 135: Proposition 23.3
Any root system is uniquely a union of irreducible ones.

Properties of the Cartan matrix: (i) $a_{ii} = 2$; (ii) $a_{ij}$ is a nonpositive integer for $i \neq j$; (iii) $a_{ij}a_{ji} \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ for $i \neq j$; (iv) the matrix $d_i a_{ij}$ is symmetric and positive definite.

## Statement 137: Proposition 23.6
The Cartan matrix determines the root system uniquely.

## Statement 138: Theorem 23.7
(i) Connected Dynkin diagrams are classified by the list $A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n, G_2, F_4, E_6, E_7, E_8$. (ii) Every matrix satisfying the conditions of Proposition 23.4 is a Cartan matrix of some root system.

## Statement 139: Theorem 24.1
(Serre relations) (i) The elements $e_i$, $f_i$, $h_i$, i = 1, ..., r generate $\mathfrak{g}$. (ii) These elements satisfy the Serre relations: $[h_i, h_j] = 0, [h_i, e_j] = a_{ij}e_j, [h_i, f_j] = -a_{ij}f_j, [e_i, f_j] = \delta_{ij}h_i$, $(ade_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j = 0, (adf_i)^{1-a_{ij}}f_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$.

## Statement 140: Theorem 24.2
(Serre) (i) The Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak{n}_+$ generated by $e_i$ has the Serre relations as defining relations. (ii) $\mathfrak{g}(R)$ is a sum of finite dimensional modules over every simple root subalgebra. (iii) $\mathfrak{g}(R)$ is finite dimensional. (iv) $\mathfrak{g}(R)$ is semisimple and has root system R.

## Statement 141: Lemma 24.3
(i) The Lie algebra $\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}_+}$ is free on the generators $e_i$ and $\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}_-}$ is free on the generators $f_i$. (ii) $h_i$ are linearly independent in $\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}$.

## Statement 142: Corollary 24.4
Isomorphism classes of simple Lie algebras over $\mathbf{k}$ are in bijection with Dynkin diagrams $A_n$, $n \geq 1$, $B_n$, $n \geq 2$, $C_n$, $n \geq 3$, $D_n$, $n \geq 4$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, $F_4$ and $G_2$.

## Statement 143: Proposition 25.3
Any finite dimensional representation V of $\mathfrak{g}$ has a weight decomposition. Moreover, all weights of V are integral, i.e., P(V) is a finite subset of the weight lattice $P \subset \mathfrak{h}^*$ of $\mathfrak{g}$.

## Statement 144: Proposition 25.5
Any finite dimensional representation $V \neq 0$ contains a nonzero highest weight vector of some weight $\lambda$. Thus every irreducible finite dimensional representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is a highest weight representation.

## Statement 145: Proposition 25.7

$M_{\lambda}$ has a weight decomposition with $P(M_{\lambda}) = \lambda - Q_{+}$, dim $M_{\lambda}[\lambda] = 1$, and weight subspaces of $M_{\lambda}$ are finite dimensional.

## Statement 147: Proposition 25.10
(i) (Universal property of Verma modules) If V is a representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $v \in V$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda$ then there is a unique homomorphism $\eta: M_{\lambda} \to V$ such that $\eta(v_{\lambda}) = v$. (ii) Every highest weight representation has a weight decomposition into finite dimensional weight subspaces.

## Statement 148: Corollary 25.11
Every highest weight representation V has a unique highest weight generator, up to scaling.

## Statement 149: Proposition 25.12
For every $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, the Verma module $M_{\lambda}$ has a unique irreducible quotient $L_{\lambda}$. Moreover, $L_{\lambda}$ is a quotient of every highest weight $\mathfrak{g}$-module V with highest weight $\lambda$.

## Statement 150: Corollary 25.13
Irreducible highest weight $\mathfrak{g}$-modules are classified by their highest weight $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, via the bijection $\lambda \mapsto L_{\lambda}$.

## Statement 151: Proposition 25.14
We have $P_F \subset P_+$.

## Statement 152: Lemma 25.15
If $\lambda \in P_+$ then in $L_{\lambda}$, we have $f_i^{\lambda(h_i)+1}v_{\lambda}=0$.

## Statement 153: Lemma 25.16
Let V be a $\mathfrak{g}$-module with weight decomposition into finite dimensional weight subspaces. If V is a sum of finite dimensional $(\mathfrak{sl}_2)_i$-modules for each i = 1, ..., r, then for each $\lambda \in P$ and $w \in W$, $\dim V[\lambda] = \dim V[w\lambda]$. In particular, P(V) is W-invariant.

## Statement 154: Theorem 25.17
For any $\lambda \in P_+$, $L_{\lambda}$ is finite dimensional; i.e., $P_F = P_+$. Thus finite dimensional irreducible representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ are classified, up to an isomorphism, by their highest weight $\lambda \in P_+$, via the bijection $\lambda \mapsto L_{\lambda}$. Moreover, for any $\mu \in P$ and $w \in W$, $\dim L_{\lambda}[\mu] = \dim L_{\lambda}[w\mu]$.

## Statement 155: Proposition 26.3

## Statement 156: Theorem 26.4
(Weyl character formula) For any $\lambda \in P_+$ the character $\chi_{\lambda} := \chi_{L_{\lambda}}$ of the irreducible finite dimensional representation $L_{\lambda}$ is given by $\chi_{\lambda} = \frac{\sum_{w \in W} (-1)^{\ell(w)} e^{w(\lambda + \rho)}}{\Delta}$.

## Statement 157: Corollary 26.5
(Weyl denominator formula) One has $\Delta = \sum_{w \in W} (-1)^{\ell(w)} e^{w\rho}$.

## Statement 158: Lemma 26.6
If V is a highest weight representation with highest weight $\lambda$ then $C|_V = (\lambda, \lambda + 2\rho) = |\lambda + \rho|^2 - |\rho|^2$.

## Statement 159: Proposition 26.8
(Weyl dimension formula) We have $\dim L_{\lambda} = \frac{\prod_{\alpha \in R_{+}} (\alpha, \lambda + \rho)}{\prod_{\alpha \in R_{+}} (\alpha, \rho)}$.
