# All Mathematical Statements from Noncommutative Algebra (MIT 18.706, Spring 2023)

## Statement 1: Lemma 1.17
If $\varphi: R \to S$ is a ring homomorphism and S satisfies IBN, then so does R.

## Statement 2: Corollary 1.18
Any ring admitting a homomorphism into a skew field satisfies IBN.

## Statement 3: Theorem 1.23
Suppose that every R-module is free. Then R is a skew field.

## Statement 4: Lemma 1.26 (Schur's Lemma)
If M is simple, then $\operatorname{End}_R(M)$ is a division ring.

## Statement 5: Corollary 1.27
Any nonzero map of simple modules is an isomorphism. In particular, if M, N are non-isomorphic simple modules, Hom(M, N) = 0.

## Statement 6: Lemma 1.28
a) Every nonzero ring has a simple module.
b) Every proper left ideal in a nonzero ring is contained in a maximal ideal.
c) A proper submodule N in a module M is maximal iff M/N is simple.

## Statement 7: Corollary 1.30
Every finitely generated module has an irreducible quotient.

## Statement 8: Lemma 1.33
Let $M = \bigoplus_{i \in I} M_i$ be a semisimple module, $M_i$ are simple modules. Then any submodule $N \subset M$ has a direct complement of the form $\bigoplus_{i \in J} M_i$ for some $J \subset I$.

## Statement 9: Theorem 1.34
Every R-module is semisimple iff $R = \prod_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Mat}_{n_i}(D_i)$ where the $D_i$ are skew fields.

## Statement 10: Corollary 2.2
Subquotients and sums of semisimple modules are semisimple.

## Statement 11: Proposition 2.9
M is semisimple iff any short exact sequence $0 \to M_1 \to M \to M_2 \to 0$ splits.

## Statement 12: Theorem 2.10
Every R-module is semisimple iff R is semisimple over itself iff $R = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{Mat}_{n_i}(D_i)$ where the $D_i$ are skew fields.

## Statement 13: Theorem 2.16 (Wedderburn)
Let R be a ring. TFAE:
a) R is simple and (either left or right) Artinian,
b) every R-module is semisimple and R has a unique simple module up to isomorphism,
c) $R \cong \operatorname{Mat}_n(D)$ where D is a skew field.

## Statement 14: Corollary 3.2
Suppose that k is algebraically closed and |G| does not divide char k. Then $|G| = \sum (\dim \rho_i)^2$ where the $\rho_i$ are the isomorphism classes of simple k[G]-modules.

## Statement 15: Theorem 3.3 (Density Theorem)
Let L be a simple R-module and $D = \operatorname{End}_R(L)$. Then given any finite set $x_1, \ldots, x_n, y_1, \ldots, y_n \in L$ with the $x_i$ linearly independent over D, there exists $r \in R$ such that $r(x_i) = y_i$.

## Statement 16: Corollary 3.5
If L is finite-dimensional simple over $D := \operatorname{End}_R(L)$, then there is a surjection $R \to \operatorname{End}_D(L) \cong \operatorname{Mat}_n(D)$, $n = \dim_D(L)$.

## Statement 17: Proposition 3.10
A module is Noetherian iff every submodule is finitely generated.

## Statement 18: Proposition 3.11
If $0 \to M_1 \to M \to M_2 \to 0$ is a short exact sequence and $M_1, M_2$ are Noetherian (resp. Artinian), then M is also Noetherian (resp. Artinian).

## Statement 19: Lemma 3.13
A module M has finite length iff M is both Noetherian and Artinian.

## Statement 20: Theorem 3.15 (Jordan-Holder)
Given two composition series $M_i$, $M'_i$ of M, gr(M) = gr'(M). Equivalently, the number of irreducible subquotients isomorphic to a given simple module L is independent of the choice of filtration.

## Statement 21: Corollary 3.19
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the modules of finite length over R. Then $K(\mathcal{M})$ is freely generated by [L] for (isomorphism classes of) irreducible modules L.

## Statement 22: Lemma 3.21
For $a \in R$ TFAE:
a) $a \in \operatorname{Ann}(L)$ for all simple R-modules L (i.e., $a \in J(R)$),
b) $a \in I$ for all maximal left ideals I,
c) $1 - xa$ has a left inverse for all x,
d) $1 - xay$ has an inverse for all x, y,
e) $1 - ax$ has a right inverse for all x,
f) $a \in I$ for all maximal right ideals I,
g) $a \in \operatorname{Ann}(L)$ for all simple $R^{op}$-modules L (i.e., $a \in J(R^{op})$).

## Statement 23: Proposition 4.15
If M is indecomposable of finite length, then $\operatorname{End}_R(M)$ is local.

## Statement 24: Lemma 4.16
If M is an indecomposable finite length module, every $a \in \operatorname{End}_R(M)$ is either nilpotent or invertible.

## Statement 25: Theorem 4.17 (Krull-Schmidt)
a) Every finite length module can be decomposed as a direct sum of indecomposable modules.
b) For any two such decompositions, the multisets of isomorphism classes of the indecomposable summands coincide.

## Statement 26: Proposition 5.6
A (left or right) Artinian semi-primitive ring has the form $\prod_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{Mat}_{n_i}(D_i)$, so (left or right) Artinian primitive rings are of the form $\operatorname{Mat}_n(D)$, hence simple.

## Statement 27: Corollary 5.7
Suppose R is Artinian. Then M is semisimple iff J(R)M = 0. The socle filtration on M has $M_i = \ker J(R)^i$ and the cosocle filtration is $J(R)^iM$.

## Statement 28: Corollary 5.8 (Nakayama's Lemma)
Suppose M is a finitely generated R-module such that J(R)M = M. Then M = 0.

## Statement 29: Theorem 6.1 (Akizuki-Hopkins-Levitzki)
A left (or right) Artinian ring is Noetherian. More precisely, if R is a left Artinian ring, $J = J(R)$, then $J^n = 0$ for some n and each $J^i/J^{i+1}$ is a finitely generated semisimple module.

## Statement 30: Proposition 6.4
Let R be a ring such that every finitely generated module has a projective cover. Then R/J(R) is semisimple and idempotents lift modulo J(R).

## Statement 31: Proposition 6.5
Let R be as in the previous proposition. Then the map $P \mapsto P/J(R)P$ is a bijection between indecomposable projective R-modules (up to isomorphism) and simple R-modules (up to isomorphism).

## Statement 32: Theorem 7.1 (Morita Equivalence)
Let R be a ring and $P$ a finitely generated projective right R-module. Then $\operatorname{End}_R(P)$-mod is equivalent to R-mod iff P is a projective generator. (A projective generator is a projective module P such that for every R-module M, there exists a surjection $P^n \twoheadrightarrow M$.)

## Statement 33: Lemma 7.3 (Yoneda Lemma)
The functor $Y: C \to \operatorname{Fun}(C^{op}, \operatorname{Set})$ sending $a \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}(-, a)$ is fully faithful. That is, morphisms $a \to b$ are in bijection with natural transformations $\operatorname{Hom}(-, a) \to \operatorname{Hom}(-, b)$.

## Statement 34: Proposition 7.5
Two rings R and S are Morita equivalent iff $S \cong \operatorname{End}_R(P)$ for some projective generator $P$ for R.

## Statement 35: Proposition 8.5
Let R, S be Morita equivalent via functors F: R-mod $\to$ S-mod and G: S-mod $\to$ R-mod. Then the bimodule $P = F(R)$ is an S-R-bimodule, and for any R-module $M$, $F(M) \cong P \otimes_R M$.

## Statement 36: Theorem 12.5 (Koszul Duality)
If A is a Koszul algebra, then A! is also Koszul and (A!)! = A.

## Statement 37: Proposition 13.4
$A \otimes_F A^{op} \cong \operatorname{End}_F(A)$ for any central simple algebra A over F.

## Statement 38: Theorem 14.1 (Skolem-Noether)
Let A be a central simple algebra over F. Every automorphism of A is inner, i.e., $\operatorname{Aut}(A) = A^\times / F^\times$.

## Statement 39: Theorem 14.2 (Artin-Wedderburn for CSAs)
A finite-dimensional central simple algebra over F is isomorphic to $\operatorname{Mat}_n(D)$ for a unique division algebra D (up to isomorphism) central over F.

## Statement 40: Corollary 14.3
Every automorphism of $\operatorname{Mat}_n(F)$ is inner.

## Statement 41: Proposition 14.5
Let B be a simple subalgebra of a central simple algebra A over F. Then the centralizer $C_A(B) = \{a \in A \mid ab = ba \ \forall b \in B\}$ is simple and $\dim_F A = \dim_F B \cdot \dim_F C_A(B)$.

## Statement 42: Proposition 14.6
If B is a simple subalgebra of a central simple algebra A over F, then $C_A(C_A(B)) = B$.

## Statement 43: Corollary 14.7
Every maximal commutative subalgebra of a central simple algebra A of degree n over F has dimension n over F.

## Statement 44: Corollary 14.8
Every commutative field extension $E/F$ of degree n embeds into $\operatorname{Mat}_n(F)$.

## Statement 45: Corollary 14.9
A CSA $A$ of degree $n$ over $F$ is split (i.e. isomorphic to $\operatorname{Mat}_n(F)$) iff it contains $F^n$ as a subalgebra.

## Statement 46: Proposition 14.10
A central simple algebra A over F is split iff it has a left ideal of dimension n = deg(A) over F.

## Statement 47: Corollary 14.11
A central simple algebra of degree n over F is split by a field extension E/F (i.e. $A \otimes_F E \cong \operatorname{Mat}_n(E)$) iff E embeds into A as a maximal commutative subalgebra.

## Statement 48: Corollary 14.12
The index of [A] divides deg(A), and they are equal iff A is a division algebra.

## Statement 49: Corollary 14.13
A CSA A of degree n over F is split by a field extension E/F with $[E:F] \le n$.

## Statement 50: Corollary 14.14
If E/F is a separable field extension with $[E:F] = n$, then $A \otimes_F E \cong \operatorname{Mat}_n(E)$ iff E embeds into A.

## Statement 51: Theorem 14.15
Every CSA A over F is split by a finite separable extension of F.

## Statement 52: Proposition 15.2
The Brauer group $\operatorname{Br}(E/F)$ for a cyclic Galois extension E/F is isomorphic to $F^\times / \operatorname{Nm}(E^\times)$.

## Statement 53: Lemma 16.3
A central simple algebra A over F is split by a field extension E/F iff E embeds into A as a maximal commutative subfield.

## Statement 54: Theorem 16.4
$\operatorname{Br}(E/F) \cong H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(E/F), E^\times)$ for a finite Galois extension E/F.

## Statement 55: Corollary 16.7
For a cyclic extension E/F, $\operatorname{Br}(E/F) \cong F^\times / \operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}(E^\times)$.

## Statement 56: Theorem 16.8
The period of $[A] \in \operatorname{Br}(F)$ divides the index, and has the same prime factors.

## Statement 57: Proposition 17.1
There exist well-defined reduced norm $\operatorname{Nrd}: A \to F$ and reduced trace $\operatorname{Trd}: A \to F$ for a CSA A over F.

## Statement 58: Lemma 17.5
If F is a $C_1$ field and $E/F$ is a finite extension, then E is also $C_1$.

## Statement 59: Theorem 17.6 (Chevalley-Warning)
Finite fields are $C_1$ fields.

## Statement 60: Theorem 17.8 (Tsen's Theorem)
Suppose k is algebraically closed. Then the field $F = k(t)$ is $C_1$.

## Statement 61: Theorem 17.10
Let F be a non-Archimedean local field, i.e. it's a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_p((t))$. Then $\operatorname{Br}(F) \cong \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.

## Statement 62: Proposition 17.14
Every central simple algebra over a local field F splits over an unramified extension.

## Statement 63: Proposition 17.15
If E/F is an unramified degree n extension of a non-Archimedean local field, then $\operatorname{Br}(E/F) = \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

## Statement 64: Lemma 18.1
For R a commutative ring and A an R-algebra finitely generated and projective as an R-module, the following are equivalent:
a) The map $A \otimes_R A^{op} \to \operatorname{End}_R(A)$ is an isomorphism.
b) For every algebraically closed field k and a homomorphism $R \to k$, $A_k \cong \operatorname{Mat}_n(k)$.
c) For every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} \subset R$, let $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$; the ring $A_k$ is a central simple algebra over k.

## Statement 65: Proposition 18.11
If $R \to S$ is faithfully flat, the functor sending M to its descent data is an equivalence.

## Statement 66: Theorem 18.15
a) Consider the functor F sending a commutative R-ring S to the set of isomorphisms $A_S \cong \operatorname{Mat}_d(S)$. This functor is representable and is represented by a ring $S_{\operatorname{univ}}$ finitely generated over R.
b) $S_{\text{univ}}$ is formally smooth over R. If R is Noetherian, it is faithfully flat over R.

## Statement 67: Theorem 19.5
Let R be a (formally) smooth finitely generated commutative domain over an algebraically closed field and $F = \operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Then $\operatorname{Br}(R) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Br}(F)$.

## Statement 68: Lemma 19.7
$R_S = R(t_s)_{s \in S}/(t_s s = s t_s = 1)$.

## Statement 69: Proposition 20.1
Let S be a right reversible multiplicative subset in a ring R. Then $(a,s) \sim (a',s')$ if there exist $t,t' \in R$ such that at = a't' and $st = s't' \in S$ is an equivalence relation on $R \times S$ and the map $(a,s) \mapsto as^{-1}$ is a bijection between $(R \times S)/\sim$ and the localization $R_S$.

## Statement 70: Corollary 20.2
For a right denominator set $S \subset R$, the kernel of the canonical homomorphism $R \to R_S$ is the set of elements whose right annihilator intersects S.

## Statement 71: Corollary 20.4
If S consists of regular elements, the natural map $R \to R_S$ is injective.

## Statement 72: Proposition 20.5
If S is a right denominator set (i.e. both O1 and O2 hold), then D (the diagram category) is filtered.

## Statement 73: Proposition 20.9
Assume R is a domain.
a) (Goldie) Either R is a right Ore domain or it contains a free right ideal of infinite rank.
b) (Jategoankar) Say R is an algebra over a field k. Then either R is a left and right Ore domain or it contains a free ring $k\langle x,y\rangle$.

## Statement 74: Corollary 20.12
If A is a domain of subexponential growth, then A is an Ore domain.

## Statement 75: Proposition 20.16
Every semi-primitive ring is semi-prime.

## Statement 76: Theorem 20.17 (Goldie)
If R is a semi-prime right Noetherian ring, then the set S of all regular elements satisfies (right) O1, and $Q = R_S$ is an Artinian semisimple ring.

## Statement 77: Corollary 20.18
If R is left or right Noetherian, it admits a homomorphism to $\operatorname{Mat}_n(D)$, so it satisfies the IBN.

## Statement 78: Lemma 20.21
If $N \subset M$ is a submodule, then there exists a submodule $N' \subset M$ such that $N \oplus N'$ is an essential submodule in M.

## Statement 79: Proposition 20.22
a) Let M be a Noetherian module. Then it contains an essential submodule that is a sum of uniform submodules, $E = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} U_i$, E essential and $U_i$ uniform.
b) The number of uniform summands is independent of choices and is the Goldie rank or uniform dimension.
c) Every submodule of full Goldie rank is essential.

## Statement 80: Corollary 20.23
If $s \in R$ is a regular element, then $sR \subset R$ is an essential ideal.

## Statement 81: Lemma 20.24
The preimage of an essential submodule is essential.

## Statement 82: Proposition 20.25
An essential right ideal in a semi-prime right Noetherian ring contains a regular element.

## Statement 83: Corollary 21.1
A module M has no proper essential submodules iff it is semisimple.

## Statement 84: Lemma 21.2
a) If $M \supset N \supset P$ with N essential in M and P essential in N, then P is essential in M.
b) The preimage of an essential submodule is essential.
c) If $N_1 \subset M_1$, $N_2 \subset M_2$ are essential, then $N_1 \oplus N_2 \subset M_1 \oplus M_2$ is essential.

## Statement 85: Proposition 21.5
A finite Goldie rank module contains an essential submodule which is a finite sum of uniform submodules.

## Statement 86: Theorem 21.6
Suppose M has finite Goldie rank and contains $E = \bigoplus_{i=1}^m U_i$ an essential sum of uniform submodules. If $M \supset N = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n N_i$ with $N_i \neq 0$, then $n \leq m$. If m = n, then N is essential and each $N_i$ is uniform.

## Statement 87: Corollary 21.7
If M has finite Goldie rank n, then every submodule in M with the same Goldie rank n is essential.

## Statement 88: Theorem 21.11
An essential right ideal in a semi-prime, right Noetherian ring contains a regular element.

## Statement 89: Lemma 21.12
Let R be a right Noetherian, semi-prime ring and $I \subset R$ an essential right ideal. Then the left annihilator of I is zero.

## Statement 90: Proposition 21.13
Any right ideal I contains an element x with $\operatorname{rAnn}(x) \cap I = 0$.

## Statement 91: Proposition 22.3
Let R be a semi-prime Goldie ring and S the set of its regular elements. Then if $I \subset J$ is an essential subideal, the localizations $I_S$ and $J_S$ coincide. Also, if I is uniform then $I_S$ is irreducible.

## Statement 92: Theorem 22.9 (Amitsur-Levitzki)
The identity $S_{2n}$ holds in the ring $\operatorname{Mat}_n(R)$ for any commutative ring R. Moreover, no (nonzero) homogeneous identity of smaller degree holds (assuming $R \neq 0$).

## Statement 93: Lemma 22.10 (Staircase Lemma)
$\operatorname{Mat}_n(R)$ does not satisfy a multilinear identity of degree $d < 2n$.

## Statement 94: Lemma 22.11
a) If a ring satisfies an identity P of degree d, then it satisfies a multilinear identity of the same degree.
b) If an algebra A over an infinite field k satisfies a polynomial identity $P = \sum P_d$ where $P_d$ is homogeneous of degree d, then each $P_d$ is also an identity satisfied by A.

## Statement 95: Theorem 23.1 (Cayley-Hamilton)
Let $x \in \operatorname{Mat}_n(R)$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with coefficients in a commutative ring R and $P_x(t) \in R[t]$ be its characteristic polynomial. Then $P_x(x) = 0$.

## Statement 96: Corollary 23.2
If $P_x(t) = t^n$, then $x^n = 0$.

## Statement 97: Theorem 23.3 (Kaplansky)
Let A be a primitive PI algebra over a field k with a homogeneous identity of degree d. Then it is simple of degree $m \le d/2$ over its center, which is a (possibly different) field K.

## Statement 98: Theorem 23.4 (Posner)
Let A be a prime PI algebra. Then its center Z = Z(A) is a domain. Moreover, $A \otimes_Z \operatorname{Frac}(Z) \cong \operatorname{Mat}_n(D)$ for some skew field D that is finite-dimensional over $K = \operatorname{Frac}(Z)$.

## Statement 99: Theorem 23.5 (Rowen)
Let A be a semi-prime PI algebra. Then every nonzero two-sided ideal meets the center.

## Statement 100: Corollary 23.6
A prime PI ring A whose center is a field K is a central simple algebra over K.

## Statement 101: Lemma 23.7
For $x \in \operatorname{Mat}_n(k)$, $\sum_i m_i x m_i^* = \operatorname{Tr}(x) I_n$ where $m_i$ and $m_i^*$ are dual bases with respect to the trace pairing.

## Statement 102: Lemma 23.9
Let $a_i$ be a basis of $\operatorname{Mat}_n(k)$ and $a_i^*$ be its dual basis (w.r.t. the trace pairing). Define $C_i := \tau_i(C)$ where $\tau_i$ is a linear endomorphism defined by $\tau_i(ux_iv) = vu$. Then for $b_0, \ldots, b_{n^2}$ any matrices, $\operatorname{Tr}(C(a_k, b_l))a_i^* = C_i(a_k, b_l)$.

## Statement 103: Theorem 23.11
The map $\operatorname{Mat}_n(k)^{2n^2+2} \to \operatorname{Mat}_n(k)$ sending $x_1, \ldots, x_{n^2}, y_0, \ldots, y_{n^2}, z$ to $Z_n(x_k, y_l, z)$ takes values in the scalar matrices and is not identically zero.

## Statement 104: Proposition 23.12
Let A be a semi-primitive PI algebra. Then every nonzero two-sided ideal meets the center.

## Statement 105: Theorem 23.13
If R is a semi-prime PI algebra, then R[t] is a semi-primitive PI algebra.

## Statement 106: Theorem 23.15 (Amitsur)
If R has no nonzero nil ideals, then R[t] is semi-primitive.

## Statement 107: Proposition 23.16
A semi-prime PI algebra contains no nil ideals.

## Statement 108: Lemma 23.17
Suppose that a ring R satisfies the ascending chain termination condition for right annihilators. If R is semi-prime, then every nil left ideal is zero.

## Statement 109: Lemma 23.18
A prime PI ring satisfies the ascending chain termination condition for right and left annihilators.

## Statement 110: Lemma 23.19
A semi-prime ideal is an intersection of prime ideals.

## Statement 111: Lemma 24.5
a) If GKdim(A) < 1, then A is finite-dimensional so GKdim(A) = 0.
b) GKdim(A[t]) = GKdim(A) + 1.
c) $\operatorname{GKdim}(A[a^{-1}]) = \operatorname{GKdim}(A)$ if a is central and regular.

## Statement 112: Theorem 24.6 (Warfield)
For any real $\delta \geq 2$, there exists an algebra with 2 generators whose GK dimension is $\delta$.

## Statement 113: Theorem 24.8 (Bergman gap)
There is no finitely generated algebra whose GK dimension is strictly between 1 and 2.

## Statement 114: Proposition 24.9
If A is a graded algebra generated in degree 1 and there exists d such that $\dim A_d < d$, then $\operatorname{GKdim}(A) \leq 1$.

## Statement 115: Lemma 24.10
Assume that there are at most d allowed words of length d. Then for $n \ge 2d$, every allowed word of length n has the form $w = w_1w_2w_3$ where $w_2$ is p-periodic for $p \le d$, $|w_1|, |w_3| \le d - p$, and $|w_2| \ge d + p$.

## Statement 116: Lemma 24.11
If a periodic word with minimal period p contains two equal subwords of length $p - 1$, then they are p letters apart.

## Statement 117: Theorem 24.12 (Smoktunowicz)
The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a graded domain cannot fall within the open interval (2, 3).

## Statement 118: Theorem 24.13 (Berele)
Finitely generated PI algebras have finite GK dimension.

## Statement 119: Proposition 24.17
If A is an algebra with commutative associated graded, then GKdim(M) is the dimension of the support of the gr(A) module gr(M).

## Statement 120: Lemma 24.18
For A with commutative associated graded and M a module with a good filtration, the (set theoretic) support $\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{gr} M) \subset \operatorname{Spec}(\bar{A})$ does not depend on the choice of filtration. Moreover, the class of $\bar{M}$ in $K(\bar{A}\text{-mod}_S)$ is independent of the choice of filtration.

## Statement 121: Theorem 24.21 (Stephenson-Zhang)
If A is right (or left) Noetherian, it has subexponential growth.

## Statement 122: Lemma 24.22
a) A has exponential growth iff $a_n = \dim(A_n)$ has exponential growth iff $\limsup \sqrt[n]{a_n} > 1$.
b) For a sequence a(n) of exponential growth, there exist $0 < r_1 < r_2 < \cdots$ such that $a(r_k) < \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} a(r_k - r_i)$.

## Statement 123: Theorem 24.23
Let $A = k \oplus \bigoplus_{i \geqslant 1} A_i$ be right (or left) Noetherian of right (or left) finite homological dimension. Then $h(t) = \frac{1}{q(t)}$, $q(t) \in \mathbb{Z}[t]$ where h(t) is the Hilbert series and q(t) is a polynomial whose roots are all roots of unity.

## Statement 124: Conjecture 24.24 (Polishchuk-Positselski)
The Hilbert series of a Koszul algebra is rational. Moreover, if both A and A! have finite GK dimension, then they have the Hilbert series of a symmetric tensor exterior.

## Statement 125: Conjecture 24.25 (Anick)
Assume A is right Noetherian. If both GKdim(A) and hdim(A) are finite, then the Hilbert series of A equals that of the symmetric algebra.

## Statement 126: Theorem 25.3 (Serre)
Let X be an algebraic variety over a field k. Then X is smooth iff Coh(X) has finite homological dimension, i.e. $\operatorname{Ext}^n(F,G) = 0$ for all n > d and all $F,G \in \operatorname{Coh}(X)$.

## Statement 127: Conjecture 25.6
The spectral sequence $\operatorname{HH}_*(\operatorname{Coh}(X)) \implies \operatorname{HC}_*^{per}(\operatorname{Coh}(X))$ degenerates for any dg-category of finite type over k.
