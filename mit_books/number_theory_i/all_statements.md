# All Statements: Number Theory I

## Statement 1: Lemma 1.4
An absolute value on a field k is nonarchimedean if and only if |1+...+1 (n times)| <= 1 for all n >= 1.

## Statement 2: Corollary 1.5
In a field of positive characteristic every absolute value is nonarchimedean, and the only absolute value on a finite field is the trivial one.

## Statement 3: Theorem 1.8 (Ostrowski's Theorem)
Every nontrivial absolute value on Q is equivalent to |.|_p for some p <= infinity.

## Statement 4: Theorem 1.9 (Product Formula)
For every x in Q* we have prod_{p <= infinity} |x|_p = 1.

## Statement 5: Theorem 1.16
Characterizations of DVRs: For an integral domain A, the following are equivalent: A is a DVR; A is a noetherian valuation ring that is not a field; A is a local PID that is not a field; A is an integrally closed noetherian local ring of dimension one; A is a regular noetherian local ring of dimension one; A is a noetherian local ring whose maximal ideal is nonzero and principal; A is a maximal noetherian ring of dimension one.

## Statement 6: Proposition 1.18
If alpha, beta in B are integral over A, then alpha + beta and alpha*beta are integral over A.

## Statement 7: Proposition 1.20
If C/B/A is a tower of ring extensions in which B is integral over A and C is integral over B then C is integral over A.

## Statement 8: Corollary 1.21
If B/A is a ring extension, then the integral closure of A in B is integrally closed in B.

## Statement 9: Proposition 1.22
The ring Z is integrally closed.

## Statement 10: Corollary 1.23
Every unique factorization domain is integrally closed. In particular, every PID is integrally closed.

## Statement 11: Proposition 1.25
Every valuation ring is integrally closed.

## Statement 12: Proposition 1.28
Let A be an integrally closed domain with fraction field K. Let alpha be an element of a finite extension L/K, and let f in K[x] be its minimal polynomial over K. Then alpha is integral over A if and only if f in A[x].

## Statement 13: Theorem 2.1
Let S be a multiplicative subset of an integral domain A. There is a one-to-one correspondence between the prime ideals of S^{-1}A and the prime ideals of A that do not intersect S.

## Statement 14: Proposition 2.6
Let A be a subring of a field K, and let M be an A-module contained in a K-vector space V. Then M = intersection of M_m over maximal ideals = intersection of M_p over prime ideals.

## Statement 15: Corollary 2.7
Every ideal I of an integral domain A is equal to the intersection of its localizations at the maximal ideals of A.

## Statement 16: Proposition 2.9
Let A be a noetherian domain. The following are equivalent: (i) For every nonzero prime ideal p, the local ring A_p is a DVR. (ii) A is integrally closed and dim A <= 1.

## Statement 17: Corollary 2.11
Every PID is a Dedekind domain. In particular, Z is a Dedekind domain, as is k[x] for any field k.

## Statement 18: Lemma 2.14
Let A be a noetherian domain with fraction field K, and let I be an A-submodule of K. Then I is finitely generated if and only if aI is contained in A for some nonzero a in A.

## Statement 19: Corollary 2.16
Every fractional ideal of A can be written in the form (1/a)I for some nonzero a in A and ideal I.

## Statement 20: Lemma 2.18
Let I and J be fractional ideals of a noetherian domain A with J nonzero. Then (I:J) is a fractional ideal of A.

## Statement 21: Lemma 2.20
A fractional ideal I of A is invertible if and only if I(A:I) = A.

## Statement 22: Lemma 3.1
Localization of fractional ideals: (I+J)_p = I_p + J_p, (IJ)_p = I_p J_p, (I:J)_p = (I_p:J_p).

## Statement 23: Theorem 3.2
A fractional ideal of a noetherian domain is invertible iff its localization at every maximal ideal is invertible.

## Statement 24: Corollary 3.3
In a Dedekind domain every nonzero fractional ideal is invertible.

## Statement 25: Lemma 3.4
A nonzero fractional ideal in a noetherian local domain is invertible if and only if it is principal.

## Statement 26: Corollary 3.5
A nonzero fractional ideal in a noetherian domain A is invertible if and only if it is locally principal.

## Statement 27: Lemma 3.6
Let A be a Dedekind domain and let a in A be nonzero. The set of prime ideals that contain a is finite.

## Statement 28: Corollary 3.7
Let I be a nonzero ideal of a Dedekind domain A. The number of prime ideals of A that contain I is finite.

## Statement 29: Lemma 3.9
Let p be a nonzero prime ideal in a Dedekind domain A. If I is an ideal then v_p(I) = 0 iff p does not contain I. In particular, distinct nonzero primes have v_q(p) = v_p(q) = 0.

## Statement 30: Corollary 3.10
For each nonzero fractional ideal I in a Dedekind domain, v_p(I) = 0 for all but finitely many prime ideals p.

## Statement 31: Theorem 3.11
Let A be a Dedekind domain. The ideal group of A is the free abelian group generated by its nonzero prime ideals.

## Statement 32: Corollary 3.13
In a Dedekind domain every nonzero fractional ideal has a unique factorization into nonzero prime ideals.

## Statement 33: Corollary 3.15
A Dedekind domain is a UFD if and only if it is a PID, equivalently, if and only if its class group is trivial.

## Statement 34: Lemma 3.16
Let A be a Dedekind domain and let I and I' be nonzero ideals. There exists an ideal J coprime to I' such that IJ is principal.

## Statement 35: Corollary 3.17 (Finite approximation)
Let I be a nonzero fractional ideal in a Dedekind domain A and let p_1,...,p_n be nonzero prime ideals. Then I contains an element x with v_{p_i}(x) = v_{p_i}(I).

## Statement 36: Corollary 3.18
Let I be a nonzero ideal in a Dedekind domain A. The quotient ring A/I is a principal ideal ring.

## Statement 37: Corollary 3.21
Every semilocal Dedekind domain is a principal ideal domain.

## Statement 38: Theorem 3.22

## Statement 40: Lemma 4.4
An irreducible polynomial f in K[x] is inseparable if and only if f' = 0.

## Statement 41: Corollary 4.5

## Statement 42: Corollary 4.6

## Statement 43: Lemma 4.7

## Statement 44: Theorem 4.9

## Statement 45: Lemma 4.10

## Statement 46: Corollary 4.11

## Statement 47: Theorem 4.12

## Statement 48: Corollary 4.13

## Statement 49: Corollary 4.14

## Statement 50: Corollary 4.15

## Statement 51: Corollary 4.16

## Statement 52: Theorem 4.19

## Statement 53: Corollary 4.20

## Statement 54: Proposition 4.25

## Statement 55: Theorem 4.26

## Statement 56: Corollary 4.27

## Statement 57: Corollary 4.28

## Statement 58: Proposition 4.32

## Statement 59: Corollary 4.33

## Statement 60: Proposition 4.36

## Statement 61: Corollary 4.39

## Statement 62: Theorem 4.40
Let L be a commutative K-algebra of finite dimension (less than |K|). Equivalent: L is etale over K; L is a product of separable extensions; L = K[x]/(f) for separable f; L is a product of fields and K_sep tensor L is a product of copies of K_sep.

## Statement 63: Lemma 4.42

## Statement 64: Proposition 4.43

## Statement 68: Corollary 4.52
